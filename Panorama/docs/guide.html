Panorama

Steel pan · tenor, double second, triple cello — VCV Rack, 40 HP, plugin 2.0.0

What it is

A steel pan is one sheet of steel hammered into many note areas. Each area is tuned so it rings at three pitches at once: its note, the octave above, and the fifth above that octave (the 12th). The areas all sit on the same drum face, so when you hit one, the areas around it sing along a little. Pans are laid out in fourths and fifths, so neighbours share partials, and that faint sympathetic shimmer is a big part of the pan sound.

Panorama models exactly that. Every note area on the pan exists all the time as a group of tuned resonators, and each one listens to its neighbours. There are no samples and no voices: hitting a note that is still ringing just adds to it, the way a real pan does, so you never run out of polyphony.

Quick start

Patch a sequencer's GATE and V/OCT into the matching inputs (both can be polyphonic, so Peal's poly cables work as they are) and OUT L / R to your mixer. 0 V is C4. Each gate hits the note area nearest to the pitch. Or just click a note on the screen: it plays and becomes the selected note. With no V/OCT patched, gates play the selected note.

The pans

TENORThe lead pan, C4 to E6 on one drum. All 12 notes round the rim in the circle of fifths, their octaves straight inside, and the top five notes in the middle. Bright and short.
2NDDouble seconds, F#3 to C#6 across two drums split by whole tones (C D E F# G# Bb on the left, the rest on the right). Warmer, longer, used for chords and strums.
CELLOTriple cello, B2 to E5 across three drums in minor-third groups. Soft stick, long skirt, more body. Good for bass lines and slow chords.

Notes outside the range fold in by octaves (right-click to ignore them instead). The layouts follow the usual patterns but are simplified, since each maker's pans differ a bit.

Striking

STICKSoft rubber (dark, round) to hard (bright, more stick click). Harder hits also get a shorter contact, so they come out brighter too.
POSITIONMiddle of the note area (mostly the note itself) to the edge (more octave and 12th, and the stick knocks the neighbouring areas too).
FORCEHow hard each hit is. With VEL patched (0 to 10 V, poly), 7 V plays at the knob's level.
ROLLPan players hold long notes by rolling: rapid alternate-stick hits. At OFF, a gate is one hit. Turn it up and held gates roll, from 6 to 28 hits a second, with the two sticks slightly uneven. The ROLL input is a gate: while it is high, held notes roll (at the knob's speed, or 14 a second if the knob is off). Once patched, rolls only happen while it is high. ROLL SPD CV changes the speed.
MUTEGate input: a palm on the pan. While high, everything chokes fast.

The ring

DECAYRing time of every note, from a quarter to 2.5 times normal. Low notes always ring longer than high ones.
DAMPA hand resting on the pan: shorter and duller, but not choked.
NEIGHBOURSHow much the areas around the struck one sing along. 0 is a clean single note. Around a third sounds like a real pan. At the top the whole face shimmers. Because the sympathy depends on partials lining up, it also reacts to the tuning: a well-tuned pan rings along more.
BLOOMOn a real pan the octave swells just after the hit, because the fundamental's motion pushes energy into it. BLOOM sets how much. With high BLOOM the octave and 12th grow for the first 50 to 100 ms. The octave blooms most when it is in tune with the fundamental, so PARTIALS changes the bloom too.
GLIDEHard hits start slightly off pitch and settle as the note dies away. Negative starts flat (the default, -6 cents), positive starts sharp. Softer hits glide less.
WIDTHSpreads the notes across the stereo field by where they sit on the drum. Unpatch R for mono on L.

Tuning: deliberately imperfect

Real pans are tuned by hand with a hammer, so no note is exactly on the mathematical value. Fundamentals are usually within a few cents, octaves a bit further off, and the 12th the least accurate. Panorama gives every note its own fixed tuning error, and three ways to control it:

DETUNEHow far the fundamentals stray, 0 to 30 cents. 0 is exact equal temperament. The default 4 cents is a good, well-kept pan; 10 to 15 is a pan that needs a visit to the tuner.
PARTIALSHow far each note's octave and 12th stray from exact 2:1 and 3:1, 0 to 50 cents. This is the "sound" of a tuner more than the pitch: small values are sweet, larger values give beating, a more metallic and clangy tone, and less bloom.
RE-ROLLGives every note a new random error: a different pan by a different tuner. The errors stay fixed until you re-roll, and are saved with the patch.
FUND / OCT / 5THHand-tune the selected note (click it on the screen), ±50 cents each, added to its random error. The bottom line of the screen shows that note's total error. The little bar under each note name shows its fundamental's error: red to the right is sharp, blue to the left is flat.
TUNEMoves the whole pan up or down by up to 100 cents (a semitone).
Right-click: re-roll, clear the hand tuning of one note or all of them, or "Perfect tuning" (sets DETUNE and PARTIALS to 0 and clears the hand tuning). Also Pitch: bend the note area to the exact pitch, which retunes the struck area to the incoming V/OCT, for microtonal sequences or pitch drift from Marbles.

CV inputs

STICK, POSITION, DECAY, NEIGHBOURS, BLOOM, DAMP: ±5 V sweeps the full range. GLIDE: 6 cents per volt. DETUNE: 3 cents per volt. PARTIALS: 5 cents per volt. All add to the knob.

Starting points

Calypso leadTENOR, STICK 60%, POSITION 25%, NEIGHBOURS 35%, BLOOM 50%, ROLL OFF. Sequence 8th and 16th note lines around D5.
Strummed chords2ND, poly chord from Peal or The Changes, ROLL at 40% so held chords roll, VEL from a random source for uneven hands.
BassCELLO, STICK 25%, POSITION 50%, DECAY 70%.
Old yard panDETUNE 14, PARTIALS 30, NEIGHBOURS 60%: out of tune, clangy, ringing everywhere.
Techno useTENOR, DECAY low, DAMP 40%, hard STICK, edge POSITION, a short Detroit-style chord stab line. Push GLIDE positive for a "pinged" synthetic edge.

Under the hood

Each note area has six modes: fundamental, octave, 12th, a quiet double octave, and two short untuned "pings" for the rim and skirt.

The stick is physical. A strike is the rubber tip (a small mass on a stiffening rubber spring, with a little loss) landing on the moving steel and being pushed back off it. The note decides how long the hit lasts: a harder hit squashes the rubber more and comes out shorter and brighter, a soft tip stays on longer and rounds the sound off, and the small light top notes give way under the stick. Contact runs from about 0.2 ms (hard stick) to 3 ms (soft stick on a cello note), the range measured on real pans. STICK sets how hard and springy the rubber is.

Where you hit. The octave's nodal line crosses the middle of the note and the 12th's runs along its length, so a dead-centre hit (POSITION 0) is nearly a pure tone and moving out brings the octave and 12th in. Each hit lands at a slightly different angle.

BLOOM is the shell's own nonlinearity. The hammered note is a shallow dome, so its fundamental, octave and 12th are coupled (the 1:2:3 relation the tuner builds in). Energy flows up from the fundamental after the hit and back again: the octave swells in, more for hard hits, less when the octave is out of tune (PARTIALS). It only moves energy around, so it can never run away.

NEIGHBOURS is a symmetric spring through the steel between nearby note areas (weighted by distance on the drum face), so notes that share a partial swap energy the way a real pan's do. It is energy-correct, so it is stable however far you turn it. A shared skirt resonance and a short stick click complete it. Untested in Rack so far.
