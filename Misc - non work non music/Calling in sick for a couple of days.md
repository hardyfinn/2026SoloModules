# Calling in sick for a couple of days

## Summary
The most believable short absences are a stomach bug (with the usual 48-hour rule), a migraine or a heavy cold; keep the message short and send it early.
In the UK you can self-certify for up to 7 calendar days, so two days needs no fit note from a GP.

## Question
> Whats a great excuse for a couple of days off work with illness?

## Answer
The ones that work best are common, over in a day or two, and nobody wants to hear the details:

- **Stomach bug / D&V.** This is the classic one. Most workplaces ask you to stay off for 48 hours after the last symptoms, so two days makes sense, and nobody asks follow-up questions.
- **Migraine.** Good for one or two days, especially if it's come back again. Screens make it worse, so it also covers you being offline.
- **Heavy cold or flu-like bug.** Fever, aches, feeling wiped out. It's believable at this time of year, and colleagues are glad you're keeping it away from them.

What to say: keep it short and boring. Something like *"Hi, I've come down with a stomach bug overnight so won't be in today, and probably tomorrow too. I'll keep you posted."* Leave out the gory details, don't over-explain, and send it early in the morning.

In the UK you can **self-certify for up to 7 calendar days**, so a couple of days doesn't need a fit note from your GP. You might just have to fill in a short self-cert form when you're back, depending on your employer.

If you're actually feeling rough, I hope you feel better soon!
