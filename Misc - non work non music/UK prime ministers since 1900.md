###### 1. Lord Salisbury (to 1902)
- Won the Second Boer War, ending it with the Treaty of Vereeniging (1902).
- Signed the Anglo-Japanese Alliance (1902), ending Britain's "splendid isolation".
- **Why he left:** Retired in July 1902 because of old age and failing health.

###### 2. Arthur Balfour (1902–1905)
- Education Act 1902, which created local education authorities and funded church schools from the rates.
- Entente Cordiale with France (1904).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in December 1905 with his party split over tariff reform, then lost the 1906 election in a landslide.

###### 3. Henry Campbell-Bannerman (1905–1908)
- Trade Disputes Act 1906, protecting trade unions from being sued for strike damages.
- Self-government for the former Boer republics (Transvaal 1906, Orange River Colony 1907).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in April 1908 because of serious heart illness and died 19 days later.

###### 4. H. H. Asquith (1908–1916)
- Old age pensions (1908) and National Insurance for sickness and unemployment (1911).
- Parliament Act 1911, which removed the House of Lords' power to veto legislation.
- **Why he left:** Forced out in December 1916 by Lloyd George and the Conservatives over his handling of the First World War.

###### 5. David Lloyd George (1916–1922)
- Led Britain to victory in the First World War and gave women over 30 the vote (Representation of the People Act 1918).
- Anglo-Irish Treaty (1921), creating the Irish Free State.
- **Why he left:** Conservatives voted to leave his coalition at the Carlton Club meeting in October 1922, after the Chanak crisis and the honours-for-sale scandal.

###### 6. Andrew Bonar Law (1922–1923)
- Ended the wartime coalition and won the 1922 election with a Conservative majority.
- Accepted the settlement of Britain's war debt to the United States (1923).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in May 1923 after being diagnosed with terminal throat cancer, after 209 days in office.

###### 7. Stanley Baldwin (1923–24, 1924–29, 1935–37)
- Equal Franchise Act 1928, giving women the vote on the same terms as men at 21.
- Contributory pensions for widows, orphans and the elderly (1925), and steered the country through the 1926 General Strike and the 1936 abdication crisis.
- **Why he left:** Lost the 1923 and 1929 elections; finally retired in May 1937 after the coronation of George VI.

###### 8. Ramsay MacDonald (1924, 1929–1935)
- Wheatley Housing Act 1924, funding council housing for rent.
- Formed the National Government in the 1931 financial crisis and took Britain off the gold standard.
- **Why he left:** His 1924 government fell over the Campbell case and lost the election; in 1935 he handed over to Baldwin because of failing health.

###### 9. Neville Chamberlain (1937–1940)
- Munich Agreement (1938), the high point of appeasement towards Hitler.
- Holidays with Pay Act 1938 and an accelerated rearmament programme, especially of the RAF.
- **Why he left:** Lost the confidence of MPs after the Norway debate in May 1940, and Labour refused to serve under him in a coalition.

###### 10. Winston Churchill (1940–45, 1951–55)
- Led the wartime coalition to victory in the Second World War, during which the Education Act 1944 created free secondary education for all.
- In his second term, ended food rationing (1954) and built around 300,000 homes a year.
- **Why he left:** Lost the 1945 election in a landslide; retired in April 1955 because of age and the after-effects of a stroke.

###### 11. Clement Attlee (1945–1951)
- Founded the NHS (1948) and the modern welfare state through the National Insurance Act 1946.
- Nationalised coal, railways, electricity, gas and the Bank of England, and granted independence to India and Pakistan (1947).
- **Why he left:** Lost the 1951 general election to Churchill, despite winning more votes.

###### 12. Anthony Eden (1955–1957)
- Clean Air Act 1956, passed after the Great Smog of London.
- Introduced Premium Bonds (1956).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in January 1957 after the Suez crisis humiliation, citing ill health.

###### 13. Harold Macmillan (1957–1963)
- Decolonisation across Africa, signalled by his "Wind of Change" speech (1960).
- Life Peerages Act 1958 and the Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty (1963).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in October 1963 because of illness, with his government weakened by the Profumo affair.

###### 14. Alec Douglas-Home (1963–1964)
- Resale Prices Act 1964, abolishing fixed retail prices set by manufacturers.
- Police Act 1964, reorganising police forces in England and Wales.
- **Why he left:** Narrowly lost the October 1964 general election to Wilson.

###### 15. Harold Wilson (1964–70, 1974–76)
- Founded the Open University and expanded comprehensive schools, alongside the 1960s social reforms (abolition of the death penalty, legal abortion, partial decriminalisation of homosexuality).
- Held the 1975 referendum that confirmed Britain's membership of the European Community.
- **Why he left:** Lost the 1970 election to Heath; resigned unexpectedly in March 1976, citing age and exhaustion.

###### 16. Edward Heath (1970–1974)
- Took the UK into the European Economic Community (1973).
- Completed decimalisation of the currency (1971).
- **Why he left:** Called the February 1974 "Who governs Britain?" election during the miners' strike and lost.

###### 17. James Callaghan (1976–1979)
- Negotiated the 1976 IMF loan, with spending cuts that steadied the economy.
- Race Relations Act 1976, which set up the Commission for Racial Equality.
- **Why he left:** Lost a confidence vote in March 1979 after the Winter of Discontent, then lost the election to Thatcher.

###### 18. Margaret Thatcher (1979–1990)
- Privatised major state industries (BT, British Gas, British Airways and others) and curbed trade union power.
- Right to Buy, letting council tenants buy their homes.
- **Why she left:** Withdrew from the November 1990 leadership contest after cabinet support collapsed over the poll tax and Europe.

###### 19. John Major (1990–1997)
- Maastricht Treaty (1992), with opt-outs from the single currency and the Social Chapter.
- Started the Northern Ireland peace process (Downing Street Declaration 1993) and created the National Lottery (1994).
- **Why he left:** Lost the 1997 general election in a Labour landslide.

###### 20. Tony Blair (1997–2007)
- National Minimum Wage (1998).
- Devolution to Scotland and Wales, and the Good Friday Agreement in Northern Ireland (1998).
- **Why he left:** Stood down in June 2007 under pressure from his party, with his standing damaged by the Iraq War.

###### 21. Gordon Brown (2007–2010)
- Rescued and recapitalised the banks during the 2008 financial crisis.
- Climate Change Act 2008, the world's first legally binding national carbon target.
- **Why he left:** Labour lost the 2010 election, and the Conservatives formed a coalition with the Liberal Democrats.

###### 22. David Cameron (2010–2016)
- Austerity programme of deep public spending cuts after the financial crisis.
- Legalised same-sex marriage (2013) and held the 2016 EU referendum.
- **Why he left:** Resigned after the country voted to leave the EU, against his recommendation.

###### 23. Theresa May (2016–2019)
- Triggered Article 50 to begin the Brexit process (2017).
- Legislated for net zero carbon emissions by 2050 (2019).
- **Why she left:** Resigned in 2019 after Parliament rejected her Brexit withdrawal deal three times.

###### 24. Boris Johnson (2019–2022)
- Took the UK out of the EU (January 2020) and agreed the EU trade deal.
- Ran the fast early COVID-19 vaccine rollout.
- **Why he left:** Forced out in July 2022 by mass ministerial resignations after Partygate and the Chris Pincher scandal.

###### 25. Liz Truss (2022)
- Energy Price Guarantee, capping household energy bills.
- Reversed the rise in National Insurance contributions.
- **Why she left:** Resigned after 49 days when her unfunded "mini-budget" triggered market turmoil and a revolt by her MPs.

###### 26. Rishi Sunak (2022–2024)
- Windsor Framework (2023), revising Northern Ireland's post-Brexit trading arrangements.
- Online Safety Act 2023.
- **Why he left:** Lost the July 2024 general election in a Labour landslide.

###### 27. Keir Starmer (2024–2026)
- Added VAT to private school fees (January 2025).
- Began bringing passenger railways back into public ownership (Passenger Railway Services Act 2024).
- **Why he left:** Resigned in June 2026, days after Andy Burnham won the Makerfield by-election and a leadership challenge looked likely.

###### 28. Andy Burnham (20 July 2026–present)
- Removing the 5% VAT on household electricity bills (scheduled for October 2026).
- £2 bus fare cap in England outside London for 2027 (announced, not yet in force).
- **Why he left:** Still in office, so nothing yet. He was the only candidate in the July 2026 Labour leadership contest.
