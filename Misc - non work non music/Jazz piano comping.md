# Jazz piano comping

## Summary
Comping is how a jazz pianist accompanies a soloist with short, syncopated chord hits rather than held block chords.
Key ideas are off-beat rhythm, rootless voicings, responsive interaction and smooth voice leading.

## Question
> What is jazz piano comping?

## Answer
Comping (short for "accompanying") is how a jazz pianist plays chords behind a soloist or singer. Instead of holding steady block chords, the pianist plays short, syncopated chord hits at irregular moments, so the rhythm feels conversational and leaves space for the soloist.

Key features:
- **Rhythm:** Chords land off the beat, often on the "and" of a beat, with deliberate gaps. Classic patterns include the Charleston rhythm (hit on beat 1, then the "and" of 2).
- **Voicings:** Pianists usually leave out the root, since the bass covers it, and play the 3rd, 7th and colour tones like the 9th or 13th. Shell voicings, rootless "A/B" voicings and quartal voicings are common.
- **Interaction:** It's improvised and responsive. A good comper listens and reacts to the soloist, sometimes answering a phrase with a chord punch.
- **Voice leading:** Chords change smoothly from one to the next, moving by small steps.

Bill Evans, Red Garland and Wynton Kelly are great players to listen to for comping. Happy to suggest exercises if you play.
