# Hot Lemonade production

## Summary
A Guy Called Gerald's *Hot Lemonade* (Rham!, 1989) was co-produced with Chapter (Anif Cousins and Colin Thorpe), engineered by Adam Lesser and Lee Monteverde, and recorded at Out of the Blue and Moonraker, Manchester, in Aug/Sept 1988.
No source ties specific gear to specific tracks; Gerald's 1988–89 kit is known from period interviews, and Thorpe has only spoken about the Voodoo Ray EP sessions.

## Question
> Can you find any details about the recording of a guy called Gerald's Hot Lemonade album? I'm particularly interested in the production of the music and any studio gear involved, as it's a beautiful sounding record.

## Answer
I assumed you mean A Guy Called Gerald's 1989 album Hot Lemonade, and I couldn't find a detailed gear or studio list for it online.

What I did find:
- **Sound:** Gerald says it was much more synth-led than his earlier work, with samplers used sparingly ("the samplers were very sparse in that"). He called it his "mockery of a pop album", made after the label pushed for something poppy. ([Dream Machine interview](https://dreammachinesinterviews.blogspot.com/2024/05/interview-guy-called-gerald.html))
- **Gear he's talked about generally:** a Tascam Portastudio, an 808 and a 303 for early demos, and later an Akai MPC60, two S950s and an S1000 for live work. These aren't confirmed for this record. ([Quietus interview](https://stage1.thequietus.com/?p=30267))

Nothing I found names the specific synths, desk or studio used on Hot Lemonade.

I can start a deeper dig that covers Discogs credits, the original Rham! sleeve notes, Red Bull Music Academy lecture transcripts and forum threads. Say if you want that.

## Question 2
> Yeah, please do dig deeper. I think this is the 1 hour record by Gerald where he worked with another producer.

## Answer 2
It is the 1989 Rham! album after all, and the other producer is **Chapter**, the Manchester duo of Anif Cousins (also spelled Aniff Akinola) and Colin Thorpe. They were labelmates on Rham! and also co-produced the Voodoo Ray EP. The CD has 11 tracks and runs about 51 minutes, which fits your "roughly an hour".

**Confirmed from the sleeve credits** (via [Discogs CD, RACD1](https://www.discogs.com/release/51573-A-Guy-Called-Gerald-Hot-Lemonade))
- Released 13 March 1989 on Rham! Records (LP RA1, cassette RAC1, CD RACD1). There is also a 1988 test pressing.
- Producers: A Guy Called Gerald and Chapter. Mixed by Gerald.
- Engineers: Adam Lesser and Lee Monteverde.
- Extra keyboards by Colin Thorpe. Extra vocals by Anif Cousins. Brenda Petrie sings on "Hot Lemonade" and "Music Sweet Muzic", and Nicola Collier on "Voodoo Ray".
- Mastered by Ian Cooper at The Town House in London.

**Studio (likely but not confirmed)**
- Moonraker Studios, a 16-track room in Manchester with a Fostex 16-track tape machine. That's where Gerald, Chapter and Monteverde cut Voodoo Ray in June 1988 ([Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-guy-called-gerald-voodoo-ray), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voodoo_Ray)).
- A 2026 review ([Ultimate Music Library](https://theultimatemusiclibrary.net/2026/04/03/a-guy-called-gerald-hot-lemonade-13-march-1989/)) names Moonraker plus "Out Of The Blue Studios". No other source backs it up, and its 1h34m runtime counts the bonus remixes.
- Music Technology (April 1990) says Rham "put Gerald back in the studio" fast and calls the album "hastily-conceived", and that he never got paid because the distributor Red Rhino went under ([muzines](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/voodoo-chile/329)).

**Gear Gerald owned in 1988–89** (from period interviews, not credited to the album itself)
- Roland TB-303, TR-606, TR-808, TR-909, TR-727, SH-101 (two of them), MC-202, CSQ-600 sequencer and Jupiter-8.
- Samplers: an Akai S900, plus an S950 borrowed from Chapter, who showed him how to reverse samples ([Micro Music, Oct/Nov 1989](https://www.muzines.co.uk/articles/a-guy-called-gerald/5261)).
- Effects and sync: Yamaha SPX90, Lexicon reverb, and an SMPTE-to-DIN box. Monteverde's method was "We gated everything and compressed it."
- He bought a Soundtracs Quartz desk for home around this time. The Akai ASQ-10 and MPC60 came slightly later, so they may postdate the album.

**Gaps:** I found no source that ties a specific synth or drum machine to a specific Hot Lemonade track. Nobody names the mixing desk, nobody says which engineer did which session, and I couldn't find Chapter talking about the record. Reviews that mention 303/808/909 on certain tracks are going by ear. The most likely place to fill these in is the LP inner sleeve, or an interview with Colin Thorpe or Lee Monteverde, if one exists.

## Question 3
> Look for interviews from Thorpe or Monteverde which reference this.

## Answer 3
I couldn't find any interview where Colin Thorpe or Lee Monteverde talks about recording **Hot Lemonade**. Thorpe has been quoted at length about the **Voodoo Ray EP** sessions. I found no interview with Monteverde at all. Other people describe his role, but no published piece quotes him.

**Colin Thorpe: confirmed quotes, all about the Voodoo Ray EP** ([Red Bull Music Academy, 2017](https://daily.redbullmusicacademy.com/2017/02/guy-called-gerald-voodoo-ray-feature/))
- "All four tracks from the EP were done in a two-day period at Moonraker Studios in Manchester."
- "We could basically get in there when Lee [Monteverde], the engineer, didn't have anything else to do." He says the EP cost about £75.
- "We had two samplers, and neither me nor Lee the engineer really knew how the bloody things worked." He also says "None of us knew how to program the S-950", so Gerald used the reverse button.
- He says Lee cut off the end of "rage" in the Peter Cook and Dudley Moore sample by mistake, which is how it became "Voodoo Ray".
- "We had to put everything on tape." Gerald ran all his machines at once for about six minutes, and the samples were played over the top by hand.
- "Lee then set the mix up." He describes Lee as "a big fan of Shep Pettibone and the Pet Shop Boys" who wanted a "clean, polished, modern, digital sound".
- On the mixdown: "The desk was a bit old and knackered, so sometimes it would click."

In the same article, Aniff Akinola (formerly Cousins) says Moonraker was owned by Mike Harding, did folk sessions by day and house at night, and that "It was all mixed live, in real time. You'd have six hands over the desk."

**Lee Monteverde: no quotes of his own, only others describing him**
- Gerald in RBMA: "Lee, the engineer, was really my first teacher in the studio," and "Lee would spend ages really cleaning all the sounds up."
- Gerald in [Sound On Sound](https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/classic-tracks-guy-called-gerald-voodoo-ray): "We gated everything and compressed it." It was Lee who suggested getting the session singer Nicola to sing over the track.

**Hot Lemonade facts from the sleeve credits, not interviews** ([Discogs](https://www.discogs.com/release/42887-A-Guy-Called-Gerald-Hot-Lemonade))
- "Recorded at Out of the Blue and Moonraker, Manchester Aug/Sept 1988." So two studios were used, not just Moonraker.
- Engineers: Adam Lesser and Lee Monteverde. Mixed by Gerald. Produced by Gerald and Chapter. Colin Thorpe played additional keyboards. Mastered by Ian Cooper at the Town House, London.
- No source says which engineer worked at which studio or on which track.

**Inference, not confirmed:** Sound On Sound mentions a Soundtracs Quartz desk next to its Hot Lemonade section, but as part of Gerald's upgraded home setup. Nothing says it was used on the album, and Thorpe's "knackered desk" comment is about Moonraker during the EP.

**Leads that aren't online:** the MDM Archive holds an [NME article from 8 July 1989](https://www.mdmarchive.co.uk/artefact/25510/A_GUY_CALLED_GERALD_CHAPTER__THE_VERSE_ANIFF_AKINOLA_COLIN_THORPE_PRESS_1989) featuring Thorpe and Akinola, but only its catalogue record is online, not the text. The RBMA quotes also seem to come from fuller interviews that were never published. Contacting Rham! Records or the archive directly is probably the only way to get anything more about the album.
