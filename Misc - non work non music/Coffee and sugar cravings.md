# Coffee and sugar cravings

## Summary
Morning coffee can drive sugar cravings later through the caffeine dip, blood sugar swings, appetite rebound, habit and poorer sleep.
Eating protein first, delaying and limiting coffee, and planning a mid-afternoon snack help.

## Question
> Why does drinking a cup of coffee in the morning make me crave sugary sweets and biscuits through the day? And is there anything I can do to lessen this effect?

## Answer
Coffee itself isn't sugary, so the cravings usually come from a few overlapping effects:

- **Energy dip:** Caffeine masks tiredness rather than removing it. When it wears off (roughly 4–6 hours later), you can feel a crash and your body seeks quick energy, which means sugar.
- **Blood sugar swings:** Caffeine can briefly reduce insulin sensitivity and raise cortisol and adrenaline. That can push glucose up and then down, and the dip drives cravings. This is more pronounced if you drink it on an empty stomach.
- **Appetite and habit:** Coffee can blunt hunger early on, so you eat less at breakfast and rebound later. Many people also pair coffee with sweet things, so the cue becomes learned.
- **Sleep:** Caffeine late in the day or an early large dose can worsen sleep, and poor sleep reliably increases sugar cravings.
- **Taste:** Bitter coffee can make sweet foods taste more appealing by contrast.

**What can help:**
1. Eat breakfast with protein and fibre (eggs, yoghurt, oats) before or with your coffee, not after.
2. Delay coffee by 60–90 minutes after waking, or have it with food.
3. Keep it to one or two cups and stop by early afternoon, so sleep isn't affected.
4. Have a planned protein-rich snack mid-afternoon (nuts, cheese, Greek yoghurt) before the crash hits.
5. Drink water alongside coffee, since mild dehydration feels like cravings.
6. Go easy on sugar in the coffee itself, which sets up the swing.
7. Try a trial week with half-caf or decaf to see whether caffeine is really the cause.

If the cravings are intense or come with shakiness or fatigue, it's worth mentioning to a GP.
