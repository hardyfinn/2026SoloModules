# UK inheritance tax changes 2024-2026

## Summary
The 40% rate and the £325k/£175k allowances are unchanged (frozen to April 2031), but farm and business relief is capped at £2.5m from April 2026, AIM relief is halved and non-dom rules now use residence.
From 6 April 2027 unused pension pots count towards the estate.

## Question
> Have there been any changes in uk inheritance tax in the last 2 years?

## Answer
Yes, quite a lot has changed. The 40% rate hasn't moved, but most of the rules around it have. Here are the main points:

- **Already in force:** The £325k and £175k allowances are now frozen until April 2031. Since April 2025, non-doms are taxed based on how long they've lived in the UK, not on domicile. Since April 2026, farm and business property gets full relief only on the first £2.5m per person, and that allowance can pass to a spouse. AIM shares now get only half relief.
- **Coming next:** From 6 April 2027, unused pension pots will count towards your estate. For most people, this is the change that matters most.
- **Last week's Budget:** It made only technical tweaks, including a trust anti-avoidance rule and fixes to the pension rules.

#### UK inheritance tax changes, November 2024 to November 2026

*Checked 3 November 2026 against GOV.UK and HMRC pages. This is a summary, not tax advice.*

**Short answer:** yes, quite a lot. The headline rate (40%) and the basic allowances haven't moved, but the rules on farms, businesses, AIM shares, non-doms and (from next April) pensions have all changed. The Budget on 28 October 2026 only made smaller, technical tweaks.

##### Already in force

**Allowances frozen for longer.** The nil-rate band stays at £325,000 and the residence nil-rate band (for a home left to children or grandchildren) at £175,000. The October 2024 Budget extended the freeze to April 2030, and the November 2025 Budget extended it again to **5 April 2031**. Because prices and house values keep rising, more estates get pulled into tax each year. ([Budget 2025 overview, para 1.13](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2025-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2025-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar))

**Non-doms: residence replaced domicile (6 April 2025).** Whether your worldwide assets are in scope for UK inheritance tax now depends on how long you've lived in the UK, not on your domicile. ([Autumn Budget 2024 overview, para 1.3](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/autumn-budget-2024-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/autumn-budget-2024-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar))

**Farms and family businesses (6 April 2026).** This was the most controversial change.
- Before, agricultural and business property relief usually gave 100% relief with no limit.
- Now, 100% relief covers only the first **£2.5 million** of qualifying farm and business property per person. Above that the relief is 50%, which works out at an effective 20% tax rate.
- The original plan (October 2024) was a £1 million cap. In December 2025 the government raised it to £2.5 million and made any unused allowance **transferable to a spouse or civil partner**, so a couple can pass on up to £5 million of qualifying property, or about £5.65 million including their nil-rate bands. ([GOV.UK announcement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/inheritance-tax-reliefs-threshold-to-rise-to-25m-for-farmers-and-businesses))
- Gifts made since 30 October 2024 count against the allowance if the giver dies on or after 6 April 2026.
- Tax due on these assets can be paid in 10 interest-free annual instalments. ([HMRC Trusts and Estates Newsletter, August 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/hm-revenue-and-customs-trusts-and-estates-newsletters/hmrc-trusts-and-estates-newsletter-august-2026))

**AIM and other unlisted shares (6 April 2026).** Relief dropped from 100% to 50%, which means an effective 20% tax rate. These shares don't get the £2.5 million allowance. ([Summary of APR/BPR reforms](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief-reforms/summary-of-reforms-to-agricultural-property-relief-and-business-property-relief))

**Environmental land (6 April 2025).** Agricultural relief now also covers land managed under approved environmental agreements.

##### Coming next

**Pensions come into inheritance tax on 6 April 2027.** This is the big one for most people. At the moment, unused pension pots and most pension death benefits pass outside your estate. From April 2027 they'll count towards it. Personal representatives will be responsible for reporting the tax and can direct the pension scheme to pay it. This is already law (Finance Act 2026). ([HMRC technical note](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/inheritance-tax-on-pensions-technical-note))

##### What the 28 October 2026 Budget did

There were no big changes, so the £2.5 million allowance, the frozen thresholds and the April 2027 pension date all stay as they were. The Budget did make a few technical changes ([Budget 2026 overview](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar/budget-2026-overview-of-tax-legislation-and-rates-ootlar)):
- An anti-avoidance rule stops certain debts tied to trust arrangements being deducted from an estate. It applies to deaths from **28 October 2026**.
- Minor fixes to the April 2027 pension rules.
- A full inheritance tax exemption for NS&I holdings affected by NS&I's tracing error.

Some news reports said the Budget made "no changes to inheritance tax". That's true of the big items, but not quite accurate overall.

## Question 2
> What is an unused pension pot?

## Answer 2
An unused pension pot is the money still sitting in a pension when you die, money you saved but never drew out or spent.

This only really applies to "defined contribution" pensions, such as most workplace and personal pensions and SIPPs, where you build up a pot of investments. If you die with £200k left in one, that £200k is the unused pot. Today it usually passes to your beneficiaries free of inheritance tax. From April 2027 it will be added to your estate, so it could be taxed at 40% if the total is over your allowances.

A few things aren't affected:
- **Final salary pensions:** These pay you an income rather than holding a pot, so they mostly stop when you die. The survivor's pension a husband, wife or partner gets from one is excluded.
- **Death-in-service payouts** from an employer are excluded.
- **Anything left to a spouse or civil partner** is still tax-free, just as with the rest of an estate.

So this mainly hits people who have a large pension pot they don't expect to spend, and who plan to leave it to children or others.
