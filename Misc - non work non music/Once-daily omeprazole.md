# Once-daily omeprazole

## Summary
Once a day, usually before breakfast, is the standard, effective and most common way to take omeprazole.
A one-off single dose helps little; antacids or alginates are faster for occasional heartburn.

## Question
> New question. Is single  dose omeprazole effective or common?

## Answer
Yes, once a day is the standard way to take omeprazole. It's effective that way, and it's by far the most common way it's prescribed.

**Once daily (the usual approach).** The NHS advice is one dose a day, usually in the morning before breakfast. That's typically 20mg for heartburn or reflux, or 10mg to 40mg depending on the condition. The drug blocks the stomach's acid pumps for well over 24 hours, so one dose a day is enough. It takes 2 to 3 days to start working and up to 4 weeks to reach its full effect. Twice-daily dosing is kept for particular cases, such as treating H. pylori alongside antibiotics or severe reflux that hasn't responded. NICE's reflux guidance (CG184) recommends a full dose for 4 to 8 weeks, then stepping down to the lowest dose that controls symptoms. For some people that ends up meaning taking it only when needed.

**A one-off single dose.** This is less useful. Omeprazole only switches off acid pumps that are active at the time, so the first dose only partly reduces acid, and the effect builds over several days. For occasional heartburn, an antacid or an alginate like Gaviscon works much faster. The over-the-counter packs are meant to be taken once a day for up to 14 days, and you should see a GP if your symptoms haven't improved by then.

Sources: [NHS: Omeprazole](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/omeprazole/) and [NICE CG184](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg184).

This is general information, not medical advice. A pharmacist or GP can tell you what suits your own situation.
