Groover

Ableton grooves for your VCV drums · 20 HP · plugin 2.0.0 · 2026SoloModules

Groover does for VCV what Live's Groove Pool does for clips. It reads a groove, which is a short clip of hits that sit slightly off the grid at different loudnesses, and moves your drum hits to match it.

Where grooves come from:

  • Live's own grooves. These are in the browser under Grooves (Core Library › Swing & Groove). They are .agr files, so drag one onto Groover's panel.
  • Grooves you extract. Right-click any audio or MIDI clip in Live and choose Extract Groove(s). In the Groove Pool, click the groove's save button (or drag it into the browser) to get an .agr file.
  • MIDI files. Any .mid drum loop works. Groover takes the timing and loudness of every hit.
  • 14 built-ins. These go from Straight and MPC-style swing (54 to 66%) to Laid back, Pushing, Wonky, Tresillo, Drunk 2 bars, a 4-bar Shuffle tide and Triplet roll.

Loaded grooves are saved inside the patch, so the patch still works on another computer.

Two ways to patch it

A · Grooved clockSend a straight 16th clock into CLOCK and drive your sequencer (Thorax, Kleener, Jack, Pam's via the NT…) from the CLOCK output. Every step moves to where the groove puts it. Groover measures the tempo, so it can fire a step early, before the real clock arrives. This is the best way to get "pushing" grooves.
B · Trigger lanesKeep your sequencer on the straight clock, but still patch that clock into Groover's CLOCK. Run up to 8 drum triggers or gates through IN 1 to 8 and take them from OUT 1 to 8. Hits are delayed to land on the groove, and gate lengths are kept. A hit can't be sent before it arrives, so early hits land on the grid. To allow early hits, turn on Lane look-ahead (5 to 40 ms) in the right-click menu. That delays all lanes by the same amount, so send the kick through a lane too to keep everything lined up.
ACCENT0 to 10 V, the groove's velocity for the current step. Patch it into a VCA, a filter or your drum voice's accent input. Following the field guide's idea, louder should also mean brighter.
LOOPA trigger at the start of every pass of the groove. Use it to reset other sequencers or count phrases.
RESETBack to step 1 of the groove. Patch it from the same reset as your sequencer.

Knobs (all have CV inputs; ±5 V sweeps the whole range)

GROOVEPicks the groove. The screen shows the number, the name (loaded files are marked *), the base grid, the length and the tempo.
AMOUNTLive's Global Amount, from 0 (straight) to 130% (exaggerated). Automate it across a build.
TIMINGHow far hits move towards the groove. This is the same as Timing in the Groove Pool.
VELOCITYHow much of the groove's loudness reaches ACCENT. Negative values flip it, so quiet hits become the accents.
RANDOMTiming wobble for each hit, up to a quarter of a step. A few percent sounds human, and more sounds drunk.
QUANTIZELanes only. Pulls loose incoming hits onto the grid before the groove is applied.
LOADOpens a file dialog. You can also drop files, or a whole folder, onto the panel.

The screen: hollow circles are the grid, filled dots show where each step lands now (orange when moved), bars show velocity, and the orange step is playing.

Right-click menu

  • Load groove file or Load a folder of grooves. A folder replaces any grooves you loaded earlier. You can then sweep GROOVE through the whole folder.
  • Save this groove as .agr for Live saves the groove with the current knob settings, so a groove you found in VCV can go back into Live's Groove Pool.
  • Show the built-in grooves as .agr files opens the folder, so you can drag them into Live and give your Live clips the same feel as your VCV drums.
  • Copy this groove's own settings to the knobs uses the Timing, Random, Velocity and Quantize values saved in the .agr.
  • Clock input: 1, 2, 4 (default, 16ths), 8, 24 (MIDI clock) or 48 PPQN.
  • Base: the grid the groove works on. By default it's taken from the groove, and you can force 1/4 to 1/32.
  • Lane look-ahead: see patch B.

Recipes

  1. Detroit tension. Keep the kick and bass on the straight clock, and put hats and percussion through lanes with Swing 16 58% at AMOUNT 80 to 100. Straight under swung is what makes it feel urgent (field guide, section 01).
  2. Steal a record. In Live, extract the groove from a Rhythim Is Rhythim or funk break, save it, drop it on Groover and run your 909 hats through it. Set VELOCITY to 100 and patch ACCENT to the hat VCA.
  3. Open the swing over a build. Send a slow ramp (Krka, or an envelope from Long Form) into AMOUNT CV, from 0 to 120% over 16 bars, then reset it at the drop.
  4. Self-mutating feel (Autechre direction). Load a folder of 8 to 10 grooves. Send a slow stepped random (Marbles-style S&H, or Lilt's SUM) into GROOVE CV, and trigger the S&H from LOOP so the groove only changes at the end of each pass. The notes stay the same while the feel keeps changing.
  5. Two feels at once. Use two Groovers on the same clock, one Laid back and one Pushing, and split the kit between them. The space between them is the "participatory discrepancy" the field guide talks about.
  6. Shuffle tide. The built-in 4-bar groove opens from straight to 64% and closes again, so your hats breathe over 4 bars without any automation.

Good to know

  • Not yet tested in Rack or with real Live files beyond two examples. The engine is tested offline: swing lands within 0.2 ms of the expected time, pushed steps fire early as they should, and gzipped and plain .agr files and MIDI files load. Live accepting the saved .agr files is untested.
  • Groover needs a steady clock to predict early hits. When the tempo changes suddenly, the first step after the change can be a little off.
  • For each grid step, Groover uses the nearest groove hit within half a step. Steps with no hit nearby stay straight and at full accent.
  • When the clock stops, on-grid steps stop with it, so nothing runs on.
