Menagerie

Sixteen engines, three macros, none borrowed

Menagerie is a macro oscillator: one module holding sixteen different ways of making sound, all played with the same few controls. It does not use any of the algorithms from Mutable Instruments Plaits or Braids. Some engines are physical (a bouncing ball, a spring, bubbles, mic feedback), some are mathematical (Xenakis's Gendy, chaos, cellular automata, bytebeat), and four are classic keyboards rebuilt from scratch (tine piano, tonewheel organ, chorus pad, harmonium).

LIFE is the same idea in every engine: how much the sound changes by itself while you leave it alone. It is there for loop fatigue. Turn it up slowly across a section and the part keeps moving without new notes.

18 HP · polyphonic up to 16 voices · plugin version 2.0.0 · MIT licence

Controls

MODELPicks the engine. The screen shows its name and what SHAPE, TEXTURE and LIFE do in it. The MODEL input adds 0.5 V per step, so a sequencer can change engines per note.
FREQ±4 octaves around C4. V/OCT adds to it and sets the number of voices.
SHAPE, TEXTUREThe two timbre macros. Each has a CV input with an attenuverter (the small knob underneath).
LIFEHow much the engine moves by itself. 0 = stays exactly where you set it.
DECAYHow long notes ring (20 ms to 8 s). For Pad it also slows the attack.
TRIGPatched: each trigger plays a note through a built-in envelope; a gate holds the note until it falls. Bubbles, Spring, Bounce and Tine are struck instead and ring by themselves. Unpatched: the voice drones, and the struck engines strike on every new V/OCT note.
LEVEL0 to 8 V (8 V when unpatched). Volume, and how hard notes are struck. Patch an envelope or velocity CV here.
MAIN, AUXAUX is a different side of each engine: a stereo partner, a raw internal signal, or a variant (see the engine pages). ±5 V.

Starting patches

Detroit stabs. Tonewheel, SHAPE 40 %, TEXTURE 50 %, DECAY 15 %. Hapax chords as poly V/OCT, gates to TRIG, MAIN and AUX to the left and right of the mixer.

Self-changing bass. Duffing or Gendy, one voice, Metropolix pitch and gate. LIFE at 30 %, then automate LIFE up to 70 % over the breakdown.

Generative bed. Bubbles or Spring with TRIG unpatched, LIFE 50 %, into a long reverb. Marbles X into V/OCT for slowly changing pitches.

Engine hopping. A sequencer's second CV lane into MODEL (0.5 V per step) so each step plays a different engine. Herd, PLL and Bytebeat work well together.

Demo (menagerie-demo.wav)

Each engine plays for about 8 seconds in F minor at 124 BPM, with SHAPE, TEXTURE and LIFE sweeping across its section. Loudness is evened out between engines.

The sixteen engines

01 Gendy

A wave made of points that take a random step every cycle, after Iannis Xenakis. Static and reedy when calm, a living, tearing buzz when pushed.

SHAPEnumber of points (3 to 32)
TEXTUREroughness: how far each point moves
LIFEthe spacing wanders and the pitch jitters
AUXsoft, low-passed copy
TrySHAPE 30%, TEXTURE 20%, LIFE 30%: a slowly breathing bass

02 Herd

Eight saw oscillators that try to fall into step. Loose, they shimmer like a chorus; pull them together and they lock into one hard unison, then drift apart again.

SHAPEspread of their own pitches
TEXTUREpull: chorus to locked unison
LIFEeach one strays and the pull breathes
AUXthe herd as sines
TrySHAPE 40%, sweep TEXTURE slowly with an LFO through 50-70%

03 Duffing

A driven spring with a nonlinear stiffness. A clean tone at low drive, then period doubling, then chaos. A growling, unstable bass.

SHAPEone stiff well to two wells
TEXTUREdrive into chaos
LIFEdamping and drive wander in and out of chaos
AUXthe spring's velocity (brighter)
TrySHAPE 80%, TEXTURE 50% on short notes

04 Scanned

A ring of 48 masses on springs moving slowly, read out at the played pitch (scanned synthesis). TRIG plucks the ring; the tone wobbles and settles.

SHAPEresting shape: sine, ramp, soft square, lumpy
TEXTUREtension: lazy wobble to fast shimmer
LIFEan invisible hand keeps nudging the ring
AUXthe ring's movement only
TrySHAPE 50%, TEXTURE 40%, LIFE 50%, DECAY long: evolving pad

05 Walsh

Additive synthesis from square-wave functions instead of sines. Clean, glassy and digital, like a 1980s organ chip; the formant sweeps sound vocal.

SHAPEformant position
TEXTUREbrightness
LIFEeach component drifts
AUXthe formant alone
TrySHAPE swept by an envelope for Detroit leads

06 PLL

A phase-locked loop like the CD4046 chip: a square VCO chases a multiple of the note. It hunts, swoops and snaps between harmonics.

SHAPEmultiple 1 to 8
TEXTUREloop speed: swoops, tight lock, buzzing ripple
LIFEthe divider slips to neighbour numbers
AUXthe XOR comparator (ring-mod grit)
TrySHAPE 30%, TEXTURE 30%, LIFE 40% on a held note

07 Bytebeat

One-line formula music: a counter running at the played pitch is turned into 8-bit sound by shifts and logic. Self-made rhythms and arpeggios.

SHAPEwhich of 8 formulas
TEXTUREshift amount inside the formula
LIFErandom bit flips mutate the pattern
AUX4-bit version
TryPatch TRIG to a clock to restart the pattern each bar

08 Automaton

Each cycle of the wave is one row of a cellular automaton (rules 90, 30, 45, 73, 110, 105, 150, 54). Each new generation rewrites the timbre.

SHAPEthe rule
TEXTUREcycles per generation: buzz to slow evolution
LIFErandom cells flip
AUXthe previous generation
TryRule 110 (SHAPE 55%), TEXTURE 40%

09 Bubbles

Bubble physics: a bubble's size sets its pitch, which rises as it nears the surface. Drops, bloops, streams and rain.

SHAPEsize spread around the note
TEXTURErise: flat blip to cartoon bloop
LIFEhow many bubble up by themselves
AUXthe stereo partner
TryTRIG from a sparse sequence, LIFE 40% for a stream underneath

10 Spring

A spring tank as an instrument: a pitched loop with all-pass dispersion, so each hit falls as a 'pew' chirp. Dub zaps, drips and metallic boings.

SHAPEdispersion: plain ring to laser zap
TEXTUREtone: dark thud to bright zing
LIFEdrips and wobble
AUXa middle tap (hollow)
TrySHAPE 70%, DECAY 60% on off-beat hits

11 Bounce

A ball dropped on a hard floor: the bounces speed up into a buzz and stop. Each impact rings at the played pitch.

SHAPEbounciness
TEXTUREthe ball: rubber, ping-pong, steel
LIFEan uneven floor
AUXthe impact clicks alone (patch to drums)
TryDECAY sets the drop height; try AUX into a drum module

12 Howl

Microphone-to-speaker feedback in a small room tuned to the note. Swells, screams and jumps between neighbouring modes like a real stage howl.

SHAPEmic distance: one pure howl to competing modes
TEXTUREamp gain: rings below 25%, howls above
LIFEsomeone walking the mic around
AUXthe mic signal before the amp
TryTEXTURE 35-45% and let TRIG clap into it

13 Tine

An electric piano: a struck tine in front of a magnetic pickup. Soft and round, or barky and bell-like.

SHAPEhammer: soft to hard bark
TEXTUREpickup: mellow to bell
LIFEstereo tremolo
AUXthe other tremolo side
TrySHAPE 50%, TEXTURE 60%, LIFE 40%, polyphonic chords

14 Tonewheel

A tonewheel organ with nine drawbars, key click, leakage, percussion and a vibrato/chorus scanner. Detroit and house stabs.

SHAPEdrawbars: 8 settings from flute to full
TEXTUREdirt: click, leakage, drive
LIFEvibrato, then chorus
AUXthe scanner swinging the other way (stereo)
TrySHAPE 40%, TEXTURE 50%, short DECAY for stabs

15 Pad

Band-limited saw and pulse with a sub, through a bucket-brigade style stereo chorus. Warm Detroit pads.

SHAPEsaw to square to thin pulse
TEXTUREchorus off, I, II, deeper
LIFEpulse-width movement and slow detune
AUXchorus right side
TrySHAPE 20%, TEXTURE 70%, LIFE 50%, DECAY 50% (slow attack)

16 Harmonium

A bellows-blown free-reed organ: puffs of air through brass reeds, with breath noise and a beating celeste rank.

SHAPEstops: 8', 8'+4', 16'+8'+4'
TEXTUREpressure: soft and breathy to loud and reedy
LIFEbellows pumping and celeste beating
AUXthe other side (celeste leans right)
TrySHAPE 70%, TEXTURE 50%, held chords
