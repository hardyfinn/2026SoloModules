# House keys: the presets behind Chicago and Detroit records, and a module to play them

**Update, 2026-11-21:** Hardy does not want the M1 sounds. Section 1.3 is kept only as a record of the search, and the proposal in part 3 is replaced by `02-revised-proposal-100-sounds.md`.

Design stage, 2026-11-21. Part 1 is the preset search Hardy asked for. Part 2 is what it means for modelling. Part 3 is a first proposal and the open questions.

Each claim is marked by how well it is supported:
- **[artist]** the producer said it in an interview
- **[press]** a magazine or reference article says it
- **[demo]** a preset-demonstration video or page matches the sound to the record, not a studio credit
- **[memory]** my own recollection, not confirmed by a page I could read today. The gear forums (Gearspace, KVR, Vintage Synth Explorer) where most of this is discussed refused automated reading, so these need Hardy's ears or a later check.

## 1. The presets

### 1.1 Yamaha DX100 and its siblings (DX21, DX27, FB-01), 1985-86, 4-operator FM

This is the Detroit box, and it is where both of Hardy's examples live. The owner's manual lists 192 ROM voices. In the first 96:

| Slot | Preset | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| B01 | **Solid Bass** | The DX27 page calls it that synth's most notable sound and says it is shared by the FB-01, DX21 and DX100 [press, Wikipedia]. "Used on numerous big tunes of the day... solid woody tone", producers mostly used presets because FM was hard to program [press, Future Music 2011]. |
| A13 | **Jazz Organ** | Confirmed as a DX100 factory voice [manual]. |
| A14 | Ham n Eggs | Organ. |
| A15 | Club Organ | Organ. |
| A16 | 6 Tease | Organ (a 60s combo organ pun). |
| B02-B05 | SyntheBass, Mono Bass, Elec Bass, Fretless | The other basses. |
| A07-A09 | OldElectro, NewElectro, High Tines | Electric pianos. |
| B11, B16 | Strings, Richstring | FM strings. |
| C01, C07 | Easy Synth, Heavysynth | Stabs and leads. |
| D21, D23 | Synvox, Voices | FM choirs. |

The second 96 (shift group) add Organ 1, Organ 2, Clickorgan, Drawbars, E Piano 1-3, SynthBass 1-2, Pluck Bass and more.

Records and people:
- Inner City, "Big Fun" (1988): "I mainly used the DX100 for the bass that I came up with in Big Fun" [artist, Kevin Saunderson, MusicRadar 2013]. He does not name the preset.
- Derrick May "used it a lot" [artist, Saunderson, same interview]; May "rinsed the Yamaha DX100" [press, Gearnews 2025]. Rhythim Is Rhythim "Nude Photo" (1987) is the track usually named [memory].
- Saunderson's wider keyboard list: Jupiter-6, DX100, Casio CZ-5000, Juno-106 [artist].
- Juan Atkins: Korg MS-10 for Model 500 basslines, and the Korg 707, another 4-operator FM synth [press, Gearnews].
- Solid Bass and Jazz Organ on Chicago records after 1987 (Trax and DJ International era, then the early 90s Chicago and New York dubs) [memory]. I could not find a page that ties Jazz Organ to a named record.

### 1.4 Roland JX-8P, 1985, analog poly (Marshall Jefferson's keyboard)

- "Every sound I get is out of the JX8P, except for piano sounds. For them I use either the JX 'Piano 2' or a real piano" [artist, Music Technology, March 1989].
- **Soundtrack** strings: Jefferson is "partial to the smooth, silky strings of 'Soundtrack'", heard on Vicky Martin "Not Gonna Do It" [press, same article]. Very likely the Ten City and Truth string sound too [memory].
- "Move Your Body" (1986) piano: Sequential Prophet 2000 sampler, played slow and sped up by the sequencer [artist].

### 1.5 Roland Juno-60, Juno-106 and Jupiter-6

- Mr Fingers "Can You Feel It" (1986): Juno-60 and TR-909, straight to cassette [press, citing Larry Heard in Vice 2017]. The chords are "an organ played like strings" [press, DJ Pierre 1995].
- "Mystery of Love" and "Washing Machine": Jupiter-6 [press, Wikipedia].
- "A key instrument in Chicago house" [press]. MK's first keyboard was a Juno-106 [artist].
- These are programmed sounds more than named presets, so they are a different job from the FM and M1 presets.

### 1.6 Others that were named

- Ensoniq ESQ-1: the only keyboard on Sterling Void "It's Alright" (1987) [artist, Jefferson].
- Casio CZ-5000: the Reese bass, "Just Want Another Chance" (1988) [artist, Saunderson]. A programmed patch, not a preset.
- Akai S900: the "Good Life" stab is a resampled chord plus a string layer [artist, Saunderson].
- "Strings of Life" (1987): sampled piano and sampled strings, no bassline [press].
- Roland Alpha Juno "What the" (the hoover) is Belgian and New York rave, outside this brief.

### 1.7 What the search says

Between 1987 and 1995 the Chicago and Detroit preset sound is overwhelmingly **4-operator Yamaha FM**: Solid Bass, the DX100 organs, the DX electric pianos and, from 1988, LatelyBass. The M1 organ and piano arrive in 1989-90 and dominate the vocal house that followed. Strings and pads come from Roland analog polys, and pianos before the M1 are samplers.

## 2. What this means for modelling

**The FM presets can be exact, not approximate.** A DX100 voice is about 70 numbers driving a known chip. The factory voice data is published as SysEx, the same format Hardy's FM4 in Fallen already loads. So realism here is not about designing a sound-alike. It is about modelling the machine precisely:
- The YM2164 operator: quantised log-sine table, the chip's envelope rate tables and stepped attack, 8 algorithms, operator 4 feedback, key and rate scaling.
- The converter: a 10-bit mantissa floating-point DAC (YM3014 on the DX100, YM3012 on the TX81Z) at about 55.9 kHz. Its quantisation is why quiet tails sound grainy and why the bass has grit that a clean FM plugin lacks.
- The analog output filter and the noise floor.
- For LatelyBass, the TX81Z's eight waveforms.
- The habits around the machine: two units detuned against each other, the DX21's chorus, playing an octave down.
The DAC detail and clock rate need checking against the service manuals before the build spec is final.

**The M1 presets cannot use the original samples**, which are Korg's. Two honest routes:
- Resynthesis fitted to recordings. Organ 2 is a short looped organ wave with a hard click at the front, played through a non-resonant low-pass and a velocity-sensitive envelope. That suits a harmonic table plus a modelled click very well, and I expect it to be hard to tell apart. Piano 16' is harder: a multisampled piano with few zones stretched across the keyboard, short loops that freeze the tone after the attack, played an octave down. I would model the piano (inharmonic partials with their own decay rates, hammer thump) and then put it through a model of the sample player (few zones, frozen loop, fixed-rate playback, non-resonant filter), because the "fake" quality is the sample player, not the piano.
- Let the module load samples the user supplies. Exact if you own the source, but it turns the module into a sample player.

**Analog strings and pads** (JX-8P Soundtrack, Juno chords) are a third engine: DCOs, a resonant filter and above all the Roland chorus.

## 3. First proposal

One polyphonic module, working name **HOUSE KEYS**, with a preset selector and a small set of performance controls. No brand names on the panel.

- **Inputs**: poly V/OCT, GATE, VELOCITY, so it plays from a keyboard or a sequencer alike. Up to 16 voices.
- **Presets (first set, 12)**: Solid Bass, Lately Bass, Jazz Organ, Club Organ, Click Organ, Old Electro piano, FM Strings, FM Voices (all chip-accurate FM); House Organ 1 and 2, House Piano 16' and 8' (resynthesis through the sample-player model).
- **Controls**: PRESET, OCTAVE, TONE (modulator level on FM, filter on the others), DECAY, VELOCITY amount, TWIN (second detuned unit), CHORUS, GRIT (converter and noise, from clean to worn), LEVEL. CV on TONE, DECAY and TWIN.
- **Outputs**: stereo.
- **Optional**: a LOAD button for a 4-operator SysEx voice, reusing the FM4 loader, so any DX100, DX21, DX27 or TX81Z voice plays through the same chip model.

A second module could follow for the analog side (Soundtrack strings, Juno chords, Reese bass).
