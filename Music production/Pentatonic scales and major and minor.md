# Pentatonic scales and major and minor

## Summary
The major and minor pentatonic scales are the major and minor scales with the two tension notes removed.
Explains which notes are dropped and why the scales sound safe over chords.

## Question
> Tell me about the pentatonic major and minor scales, and how they relate to the major and minor scales.

## Answer
The pentatonic scales are the major and minor scales with the two "awkward" notes taken out. Five notes instead of seven.

**Major pentatonic = major scale minus the 4th and 7th**
- C major: C D E F G A B
- C major pentatonic: C D E _ G A _ (degrees 1 2 3 5 6)

**Minor pentatonic = natural minor minus the 2nd and 6th**
- A minor: A B C D E F G
- A minor pentatonic: A _ C D E _ G (degrees 1 ♭3 4 5 ♭7)

**Why those notes?** The removed notes are the ones a semitone away from a neighbour (E–F and B–C in C major). Semitones create tension: the 4th wants to fall to the 3rd, the 7th wants to rise to the root. Take them out and no two notes clash, so every note sounds fine over almost anything. That's why pentatonics show up in blues, funk, soul, reggae, folk music from all over the world, and every "you can't play a wrong note" lesson.

**Major and minor pentatonic are the same five notes.** C major pentatonic (C D E G A) and A minor pentatonic (A C D E G) share every note. Only the home note changes, just like C major and A minor. The black keys on a piano are G♭ major / E♭ minor pentatonic.

**What this means for you**
- **They fit more chords.** Minor pentatonic fits Dorian, natural minor and Phrygian. Major pentatonic fits major, Mixolydian and Lydian. So a pentatonic line will sit over most chords the Hapax gives you without fighting them.
- **Ideal for generative patches.** With no semitones, random notes never land on an ugly clash. Quantise Marbles, Random8 or Metropolix to minor pentatonic and you can push the randomness much harder before it sounds wrong. Use the disting NT quantizer or Marbles' built-in scales, or Live's Scale MIDI effect on the Live side.
- **Detroit chords are hiding in it.** Stack A minor pentatonic notes and you get Am7 (A C E G) and Am11 (A C D E G). Those are the lush minor 7th and 11th chords at the heart of a lot of Detroit pads and stabs.
- **Adding notes back gives you colour.** Minor pentatonic + ♭5 = the blues scale (the "blue note"). Add the 2nd or 6th back one at a time and you're moving toward Dorian or Aeolian. That's a nice way to bring in tension at a chosen moment in an arrangement, such as a sequencer that switches from pentatonic to the full scale at the drop.

Quick experiment: run one Marbles line quantised to minor pentatonic and another to full Dorian on the same root, and switch between them every 8 bars. You'll hear the tension come and go.
