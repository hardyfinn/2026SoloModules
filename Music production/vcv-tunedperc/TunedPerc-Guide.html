TUNED PERC

One drum sample, tuned to C and played from V/OCT. A VCV Rack module by Haer.

1. Install

  1. Drop TunedPerc-2.0.0-mac-arm64.vcvplugin into ~/Library/Application Support/Rack2/plugins-mac-arm64/.
  2. Restart Rack. Tuned Perc is under the brand Haer.

Newer builds always appear at github.com/hardyfinn/tunedperc, Releases, latest.

2. First sound in one minute

  1. Right-click and choose Load sample..., or drag a tom, 808, rim or cowbell from Finder onto the module. WAV and AIFF both work.
  2. The screen shows what it heard, for example E2+12c, how sure it is (sure 92%), and the C it moved it to: C2, -4.12 st.
  3. Patch a clock into TRIG and OUT to your mixer.
  4. Flick TUNE between to C and as is to hear the difference.

3. Playing in key

After tuning, the drum sits on a C. V/OCT moves it from there, exactly like an oscillator:

V/OCTPlays
0 Vthe drum's own C (C2 for the tom above)
+0.25 V (3 semitones)D#
+0.583 V (7 semitones)G, a fifth up
+1 VC an octave up
-1 VC an octave down

Why 0 V is the drum's own C and not C4: a tom detected at E2 stays near E2, so its body and decay still sound natural. If 0 V meant C4, the same tom would be pushed up two octaves and lose its weight. Use OCTAVE when you do want it higher or lower.

Tracks not in C: send the same pitch CV that plays your bass. If the sequence is written in A minor, an A from the sequencer plays an A on the drum. To make a drum's resting note the root of your key without a sequencer, use a constant offset (for example VCV 8vert or an Offset module) into V/OCT: 9/12 V = A, 5/12 V = F, and so on.

NOTE out works the other way: it sends the pitch the drum is playing as standard V/OCT (0 V = C4), so you can tune a synth to the drum. With TUNE on as is, NOTE is simply the drum's natural pitch.

4. Panel tour

ControlWhat it does
OCTAVE-3 to +3 octaves, in whole steps.
FINE-50 to +50 cents, to match a detuned synth or to correct the detector.
DECAY10 ms to 4 s. Fully CW plays the whole sample.
LEVELSilent to +6 dB, with 0 dB at the default (about 2 o'clock).
TUNEto C uses the detected pitch. as is plays the sample as recorded (V/OCT still works).
PITCHhold reads V/OCT when each hit starts, like a drum machine. track lets the hit follow V/OCT while it rings, so a slewed CV makes the drum glide.
DETECTListens again. It lights while working. Use it after changing Listen to in the right-click menu.
TRIG / V/OCT / VELTrigger, pitch (1 V/oct) and velocity (0-10 V, unpatched = full).
OUT / ENV / NOTEAudio (5 V peak, stereo files mixed to mono), the hit's envelope (0-10 V), and the note playing as V/OCT.

5. Reading the screen

  • Top line: the file name and how sure the detector is. Under 40% it turns red and the sample plays as recorded.
  • Big line: the detected note and cents, an arrow, the C it is moved to, and the shift in semitones.
  • Waveform: the teal band is the part the detector listened to. The yellow line is the pitch over time, with an octave above and below filling the height. A flat line means a steady drum, and a falling line is an 808-style glide. The white line is the playhead.
  • Bottom line: the note playing right now (including V/OCT), and the OCTAVE and FINE settings.

6. When the detection is wrong

  • "no clear pitch": hats, snares, claps and shakers are mostly noise, so there's no single note to find. They play as recorded, and V/OCT still transposes them.
  • The note sounds wrong on a gliding kick: right-click, go to Listen to, and try The attack or The whole hit. The default, Settled pitch, uses the end of the glide, which is usually what we hear as an 808's note.
  • A little off: trim it with FINE. Big shifts sound less natural, so the tuning never moves a drum more than 6 semitones.
  • Rack's log.txt lists each sample's detected note and confidence.

7. Starter patches

DETROITToms that follow the chords

  1. Three Tuned Percs with low, mid and high toms, each tuned to C.
  2. Patch the chord root from your sequencer (or a Metroplex lane) into all three V/OCT inputs. Set OCTAVE so they sit in different registers.
  3. Trigger them from different Euclidean lanes. The tom fills now change note with the harmony, the way tuned toms do on classic Detroit records.

BASS808 bassline

  1. Load an 808 kick with a long tail. DECAY fully CW, PITCH on track.
  2. Sequencer pitch through a slew limiter into V/OCT, and gates into TRIG. Notes that change while the tail rings now glide.
  3. Patch ENV into a VCA's CV (inverted) on your pads to duck them under each note.

AUTECHRERandom notes, locked to a scale

  1. A metallic perc or bell sample. A random source (Marbles-style, or VCV Random) through a quantiser set to your scale, into V/OCT.
  2. Trigger it from Randrum's HIT or SKIP output, so it answers the drum pattern.
  3. Bring FINE a few cents off and run a second Tuned Perc with the same sample for a detuned, chorused pair.

REVERSETune the synth to the drum

  1. TUNE on as is. Patch NOTE into a VCO's V/OCT.
  2. The oscillator now plays the drum's natural pitch, ready to layer as a sub or a drone under the hit.

From Ableton

Play Tuned Perc from a Live MIDI clip with VCV's MIDI-CV (V/OCT into V/OCT, GATE into TRIG, VEL into VEL), or from CV Tools through the disting NT. Write the drum part in the same clip key as your bassline, and the drums follow it.
