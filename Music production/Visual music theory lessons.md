# Visual music theory lessons

## Summary
Sample lessons that teach chord building, modal interchange and metric modulation visually, without the stave.
A follow-up adds a lesson on tritone substitution.

## Question
> I'm wondering if you could build a few sample lessons for a music theory course. I'm not very interested in looking at the stave, but I am interested in learning about constructing chords, modal interchange, metric modulation. I'd like to be able to do this visually, and I find most music courses involving the stave very boring. Please mock up or think about how you could present this.

## Answer
I've put three sample lessons on one interactive page called **Shape & Pulse**. There's no stave anywhere, and every diagram plays sound when you click it.

- **Building chords:** the 12 notes sit round a clock face, and a chord is a shape on it. Changing the key just rotates the shape. The same chord also shows as stacked blocks in a piano roll. Colours show how far each note is from the root, and you can click notes on the wheel to build your own chords.
- **Modal interchange:** a ladder of the six modes on one root, from bright to dark, with the notes that differ lit up. Below it you drop chords into a 4-chord loop. It starts as Cm, A♭, Fm, B♭, and swapping in Dorian's F major shows what one borrowed chord does. There are presets for Phrygian, Lydian and Mixolydian too.
- **Metric modulation:** an animated pulse grid. One "pivot" pulse keeps the same spacing across the bar line while the beat changes around it. The first preset goes from 128 to 170.7 BPM by making the dotted 8th the new beat. The Ghost option keeps the old tempo running underneath, like the GHOST output on your Pivot module.

Each lesson ends with a short exercise for the Hapax, VCV or Live. The bottom of the page compares three ways to deliver the whole course: web lessons, Max for Live devices, or VCV patches. I'd go with **web lessons, each ending with a small VCV patch or Live set**.

Two questions when you've tried it:
1. Does the wheel-and-piano-roll view work for you, or would you rather see everything as piano roll, like in Live?
2. Which strand should the course build out first?

## Question 2
> Please show one more lesson on tritone substitution.

## Answer 2
Lesson 4, **tritone substitution**, is now on the same Shape & Pulse page, after the metric modulation lesson.

- **On the wheel:** G7 is drawn as a solid shape and its substitute, D♭7, as a dashed shape half way round. A thick line through the centre joins B and F, the tritone the two chords share.
- **In the piano roll:** the ii–V–I is laid out with the shared notes outlined. A dashed line follows the bass, so you can see it jump D, G, C in the original and slide down D, D♭, C with the sub.
- **Sound:** "A/B both versions" loops the original and then the substitute, so you can hear the difference.
- **Options:** you can pick any home key, and a Minor setting lands on Cm9.
- **Take it to the rig:** add 0.5 V to the root CV on the V bar, transpose the chord by 6 on the Hapax, or move only the bass note in Live.
