# Modal interchange

## Summary
Modal interchange borrows chords from a parallel mode with the same root.
A practical move is to change one chord every 8 bars without changing the bass root.

## Question
> What is modal interchange?

## Answer
Modal interchange (or modal mixture) is borrowing chords from a parallel mode, meaning one with the same tonic, while you stay in your home key. In C major you might borrow from C minor: the iv (F minor), bVI (A♭), bVII (B♭) or bIII (E♭).

Why it works: you keep your home tonic, but a borrowed chord adds a darker or brighter colour, and it needs no modulation. The classic sound is a major progression dipping to iv, as in C – F – Fm – C.

For your music with Hapax chords, try these:
- **Swap one chord:** play your minor loop, then replace one chord with its parallel-major version, such as a major IV in a minor key (Dorian colour).
- **Borrow the bVII:** an easy lift in techno, which reads as Mixolydian or Aeolian depending on the key.
- **Use it as an arrangement move:** change one chord every 8 bars to break loop fatigue without changing the bass root.
