Comper Fable 2.5.0 · VCV Rack 2

A jazz quartet in four modules

Comper plays piano chords, Walker plays upright bass, Rider plays drums and Soloist plays a horn. Put them next to each other and they find each other without cables. They share one clock and one chart, listen to each other and play a whole tune together.

The row

38 HPComperPiano player. Owns the clock and the chart. Notes out, for Joanna or any poly voice.
36 HPWalkerBass player on a modelled upright. Audio and notes out.
30 HPRiderDrummer on a modelled jazz kit. Stereo and triggers out.
30 HPSoloistHorn player: tenor sax, trumpet or trombone. Stereo and notes out.
the link: touching modules pass it along, in any order

Panels drawn to scale by width. The link travels through Rack's expander connection, so the modules must touch. Any order works, and other modules can't sit in between.

Getting a tune going

  1. Place Comper, then Walker, Rider and Soloist touching it in a row.
  2. Patch Comper's V/OCT, GATE and VEL into a polyphonic piano. Joanna is the intended partner; its PEDAL input takes Comper's PEDAL out.
  3. Patch Walker's OUT, Rider's L/R and Soloist's L/R into a mixer.
  4. Pick a progression on Comper's screen (the arrows step through all 44; tap the name for a list by genre), pick a GROOVE and press RUN.
  5. Everyone starts on the same beat. Walker and Rider follow Comper's groove while their FEEL is at 0. Soloist plays the head, solos, trades, plays the head out, and the band ends on the last chord.

Each module still works alone. Without Comper, Walker, Rider and Soloist each take a CLOCK and RESET cable (pulses per beat in the right-click menu). Walker and Soloist can also take chords from a cable.

The players

Comper: piano

38 HP · notes out

Comper comps the way a jazz pianist accompanies. It voices chords with the smallest moves between them and plays swung two-bar rhythm figures that push into the next chord. Its hands are loose and human, and it leaves room for a soloist. It makes notes, not sound, so patch it into a piano.

Soloist: horn

30 HP · stereo and notes out · new in 2.5.0

Soloist makes the trio a quartet. It writes a head melody from the loaded chart: long guide tones, a two-bar rhythm motif, and a last note on the tonic. It solos in phrases that breathe and build, trades with the drums and the piano, plays the head out and ends with the band. The progression's genre colours the line: blues and gospel bend thirds, funk plays short sixteenths, swing tongues the off-beats.

Who hears what

Each module tells the row a little about itself, and the others react. Nothing needs patching.

FromSendsWho reacts, and how
ComperClock, reset, the chart with its key, the groove and swing, the progression's genre, and the comping figure it is about to playEveryone runs on Comper's clock and chords. Walker and Rider follow the groove when FEEL is 0. Rider catches the piano's pushes. Soloist writes its head and lines over the chart, in the genre's style.

A tune from start to finish

FORM at Tune, CHORUSES 2, TRADE twos, WITH both. Each block is one bar. The chorus here is kept to four bars so the whole tune fits; in Rack a chorus is the chart, repeated until it is at least 32 beats long. The last chord is held for a bar, then the band stops.

Horn plays Band plays Drum solo Piano solo Lays out or rests
  • Head: the horn plays the melody it wrote from the chart. Same seed, same tune.
  • Solos: phrases of one to four bars with rests between. Energy starts low, peaks about two thirds through and eases off.
  • Trading: the horn and its partner alternate, always changing on a bar line. With WITH set to both, the partner alternates between drums and piano.
  • Head out: the head again, ending on a held tonic over the last chord. A bar later the band stops.
  • The FORM output fires at each change of part and PHRASE at the start of each phrase, for driving lights, filters or other sequencers.

Two Soloists

Put a second Soloist in the row. The one further left leads: its FORM, CHORUSES and TRADE settings run the tune, and the second follows them. Set SECTION on the leader.

Horns

A horn section. The second plays the head with the leader a third or sixth below, choosing notes that belong to the chord. They take solo choruses in turn.

Turns

Two soloists sharing the stand. The leader plays the head alone, they alternate solo choruses, and in the trading section they trade with each other and the drums: leader, partner, second, partner.

Tenor and trumpet make the classic front line. Tenor and trombone sit lower and darker. Give each a different SEED so their lines differ.

Starting points

Blowing on the blues

Comper on “Jazz blues in F”, Swing, about 160 bpm. Soloist on tenor, CHORUSES 2, TRADE fours WITH drums, OUTSIDE 0.1. Rider ENERGY 0.6, FILLS 0.5.

Late-night ballad

Comper on “Ballad”, Ballad groove, 66 bpm, DENSITY 0.4. Soloist on tenor, CHORUSES 1, TRADE off, ENERGY 0.4, SPACE 0.5, ROOM up. Rider switches to brushes.

House with a trumpet

Comper on “House: piano lift”, House groove, 122 bpm. Soloist on trumpet, TRADE eights WITH piano: Comper takes eight-bar piano breaks.

Out

Comper on “Modal two-chord”, fast swing. Soloist FORM Solo, OUTSIDE fully up, ENERGY 0.85. Comper and Walker follow the horn further out; Rider plays harder.

Horn on cue

Soloist FORM Cue with a gate into PLAY. The horn starts a solo when the gate rises and stops when it falls. Fire TRADE to start trading at the next bar.

Notes for another voice

Soloist HORN Off. Use V/OCT, GATE, VEL and BREATH to play a synth or a sampler; BREATH works well on a filter or a VCA.

Listening

Ten full-band demos are in the project's vcv-soloist/quartet-demos folder, rendered offline with the same code that runs in Rack and Joanna on piano. They cover blues, major and minor jazz, bossa, ballad, house, soul, free playing, two horns in each SECTION mode, and reggae. Horn-only demos are in vcv-soloist/horn-demos.
