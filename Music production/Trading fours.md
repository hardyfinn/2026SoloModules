# Trading fours

## Summary
Trading fours is when soloists take turns for four bars each, often with the drummer.
The answer also gives ways to use the idea in electronic music.

## Question
> Tell me about "trading fours"

## Answer
Trading fours is a jazz habit where soloists take turns in four-bar chunks. Usually it's a horn and the drummer: the sax plays four bars, the drums answer with four, back and forth. Each reply picks up something from the last phrase, such as a rhythm, a motif or a mood, and twists it, so it works like a conversation. Trading eights or twos works the same way, and shorter trades feel more heated. Listen to Max Roach or Art Blakey trading with horn players for good examples.

It turns up outside jazz too. Funk and soul have call and response, with James Brown cueing the band. Dub soundclashes and hip hop have DJs or MCs going back to back. In techno, two sequences can take turns.

**For your music:** it's a ready-made answer to loop fatigue. Two voices take turns being the focus, and each one varies what the other just did, so the track keeps moving without new material.

**Patch it today:**
- Use a clock divider to make a gate that flips every 4 bars.
- Let it switch which voice is muted, or which sequencer is active.
- Have Marbles or K-Accumulator mutate the pattern only at each handover.

A module could go further and actually "listen" to one part's phrase, then generate a reply.
