Dung Revisions

A VCV Rack module for learning your clock divider / Rungler module while you're away from the case. It has the same ideas and roughly the same layout, plus a display that shows the register, its voltages and every division as they happen.

Preview mock-up: the display shows example data; in Rack it updates live.

1. Install

Download DungRevisions-2.0.0-mac-arm64.vcvplugin from the latest release (or from the project's Library). Put it in ~/Library/Application Support/Rack2/plugins-mac-arm64/ and restart Rack. It appears under the brand Haer.

2. First sound in one minute

  1. Add VCV Clocked (or any clock) and patch a 16th-note clock into Clock.
  2. Out of the box, /4 goes to Bus 1 and /3 to Bus 2. Bus 1 clocks the register, so it steps on every beat.
  3. Flick Write up to High. On the next step a 1 enters the register and the switch springs back. You'll see a lit cell walk along the 8 stages on the display and come back round, because Length is 8 and Chance is fully clockwise (locked).
  4. Patch 3-Bit into a quantiser and then a VCO's V/Oct, and Bus 2 into a drum. You now have a looping melody against a 3-against-4 rhythm.
  5. Patch Noise into Data and turn Chance down a little. The pattern starts to mutate. Turn it fully clockwise again and whatever is in the register now locks.

3. The panel, section by section

/2 … /8Divisions of the clock. Each output is high only while the clock is high, so the input's pulse width carries through. Use triggers in for triggers out, or gates in for gates out.
3-way switchesUp = send this division to Bus 1, middle = nowhere, down = Bus 2. The division's own jack works whatever the switch is set to.
Bus 1 / Bus 2OR of every division sent there. When two divisions land on the same clock you get one pulse, not two. Bus 1 also clocks the register, so its rhythm is the register's speed.
Length + jackThe loop point: 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 or 16 steps. The ring of lights shows which. The jack adds 1 V per position.
Direction button + jackReverses the register. With the jack held high the direction is the opposite of the button. You can switch it in the right-click menu to "each trigger flips".
Write High / LowHigh forces a 1 into the register at the next step, Low forces a 0. It springs back by default (right-click to make it latch). Low held for a full loop clears the pattern.
Chance + jackThe heart of it. Fully clockwise ("loop"): the bit at the loop point comes back, so the pattern repeats forever. Fully anticlockwise ("new"): Data XOR the loop-point bit goes in, so every step can change. In between, a weighted coin toss picks one or the other at each step. The jack adds 10% per volt.
DataRead at each step (above 0.5 V counts as 1). Unpatched it reads 0, which means Chance does nothing until you patch something here. Noise is the classic choice, but any gate, LFO or audio works and each sounds different.
Clock / ResetReset makes the next clock count as the first one again, so every division fires together. Send it a bar pulse to keep the divisions lined up with Live.
1-BitStage 1 as a gate. A rhythm that follows the register.
3-BitStages 1-3 as 8 voltage levels. The right-click menu has ranges including 1 semitone per step and 1 octave per step, which go straight to pitch.
8-BitStages 1-8 as 256 levels. Wild and jumpy: good for filter cutoff, wavefolds, or pitch through a quantiser with a narrow scale.
NoiseWhite noise, -5 to +5 V.

Hover over any jack and the tooltip says what it is doing right now, for example "Bus 1 = /3 OR /5", "/7 next pulse in 4 clocks", or "Stages 1-3 = 5 of 7 = 5.71 V".

4. Reading the display

Shift register16 cells. Cells inside the loop are outlined; cells past the loop are dimmer (bits trail into them and fall off the end). The bars above mark stages 1-3 (3-Bit) and 1-8 (8-Bit). The line under the cells is the loop: green when the last bit came back from the loop point, blue when it was new data, red when Write forced it. The small triangle shows where new bits enter, which moves to the loop point when the register runs in reverse.
Next bitWhat the next step will put in and why, e.g. "70% loop 1 / 30% new 0".
8-Bit traceThe last 64 steps of the 8-Bit output. A locked pattern shows as a repeating shape, which is the easiest way to see the loop length.
3-Bit barsThe last 64 steps of the 3-Bit output, as a step-sequencer view.
Clocks gridThe last 24 input clocks, one row per division plus both buses. Green = went to Bus 1, pink = Bus 2, grey = fired but not sent to a bus. This is where polyrhythms become obvious.
FooterWhat each bus is made of right now.

5. Lessons and starter patches

Each one works the same on the hardware.

Lesson 1: Bus maths (Detroit hats and percussion)

16th clock in. Send /3 and /4 down to Bus 2 and patch Bus 2 into a hat. That's a rolling 3-against-4 pattern that repeats every 12 steps. Now add /7 to Bus 2 and watch the grid: the cycle stretches to 84 steps but stays groovy because /4 keeps it anchored. Patch a straight /4 jack to the kick and /8 through a long-decay envelope for a clap layer. Send a bar pulse to Reset if you want it to realign every bar, or leave it unreset for drift.

Lesson 2: Locked melody, then mutate (arrangement tool)

Noise → Data, 3-Bit in semitone steps → quantiser (minor pentatonic) → VCO, with Bus 1 = /2 (8th notes). Start with Chance at noon until you hear a phrase you like, then turn it fully clockwise. It's locked. Play the verse like that. For the breakdown, ease Chance down to about 80% and the phrase slowly rewrites itself; turn it back up to lock the new version. This answers "how do I steer it somewhere": Chance is a record / play switch for melodies.

Lesson 3: The Rungler bassline

Patch a VCO's square wave (audio rate) into Data instead of noise, Chance about 30%. The register samples the oscillator, so changing the VCO's pitch changes the pattern in a repeatable way. 8-Bit → a second VCO's FM or a filter cutoff gives the classic Benjolin chatter. Then try 8-Bit back into the first VCO's pitch: now the oscillator and the register influence each other, which is the Rungler idea.

Lesson 4: Self-mutating patterns (Autechre territory)

  • 1-Bit → Direction: the register reverses whenever stage 1 is high, so patterns fold back on themselves.
  • Bus 2 → Data: the rhythm of the divisions becomes the material for the register.
  • 8-Bit → attenuator (about 0.3) → Length: the pattern changes its own loop length.

Leave Chance at about 85%. It wanders without collapsing into noise, and returns to similar shapes. Patch 3-Bit to pitch and the 1-Bit and Bus outputs to drums.

Lesson 5: A voltage-controlled divider

Set Length 8, Chance locked, write a single 1. Now 1-Bit fires once per loop. Use it to clock a sample and hold sampling a slow LFO, and patch the S&H output into Length CV. Each time round, the loop picks a new length, so 1-Bit becomes a divider whose division changes every cycle.

6. In your rig

  • Clock from Live: CV Tools' CV Clock Out through the disting NT gives a steady 16th (or 24 ppqn divided down) into Clock. A second CV Tools trigger at the start of every bar into Reset keeps the divisions phase-locked to the arrangement.
  • Chance from Live: automate a CV Tools CV out into the Chance jack. Then "lock" and "mutate" become clip automation, which is an easy way to arrange: locked for 16 bars, mutating for 4, locked again.
  • Pitch: 3-Bit in semitone mode into Metropolix's transpose, or into Just Friends' V/Oct in synth mode for chords that change with the pattern.
  • DROID: the bus outputs and 1-Bit make good gates for DROID logic, e.g. a logic circuit ANDing Bus 1 with a Marbles gate for sparse accents.
  • Marbles: Marbles' t outputs into Data give random material that is still clock-related, which sounds tighter than noise.

7. Differences from the hardware (and guesses)

I built this from the panel photo and published descriptions, not a full manual. Where the hardware's exact behaviour wasn't stated, I chose something sensible:

  • Output voltage ranges of the 3-Bit and 8-Bit are guesses (default 0 to 8 V; change in the right-click menu).
  • Bit weighting: by default stage 1 is the biggest step ("reverse-encoded"); the menu can flip it.
  • The Write switch springs back after one step, so you don't need to hold the mouse. The hardware's switch is momentary and writes for as long as you hold it.
  • In reverse, the loop rotates the other way and stages past the loop hold still. The hardware's exact reverse behaviour past the loop point is unknown.
  • Mid Chance is a weighted coin toss between "loop" and "Data XOR loop". The hardware describes it as noisy interference, which is similar in effect.
  • Reset restarts the divisions only (menu option to also clear the register).
  • No expander ports.
