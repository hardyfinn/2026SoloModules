# Les Liaisons Dangereuses in their own words

## Summary
Quotes from Les Liaisons Dangereuses and people around them on influences, gear and stories.
The full PDF text is included.

## Question
> New thread, PDF on the musical group Les Liaisons Dangereuses. This is the 1980s synth EBM group. I'd like you to source from either band quotes from the members themselves or from those who were around them at the time that they were creating music. I'm interested particularly in influences, the gear they used, and quotes from the band themselves, stories from the band themselves.

## Answer
The Liaisons Dangereuses PDF is done: liaisons-dangereuses-in-their-own-words.pdf, 12 pages. It uses the same tags as the Derrick May one: verbatim, translated from German, second-hand, or narration.

**Who's quoted**
- **Beate Bartel**, the only band member with readable interviews. She covers meeting Chrislo Haas, holding a casting to find Krishna Goineau, and her live rig: an MS-20 and sequencer whose "tempi constantly drift apart", plus 4-track stem tapes and an effects unit, which she calls "the analogue precursor of Ableton".
- **Gudrun Gut** on Bartel's live mixing, the Plank overdubs on "Los Niños", and what they listened to in Berlin.
- **Gabi Delgado** and **Robert Görl** of DAF on Chrislo Haas, and Plank putting the DAF Korgs through guitar amps.
- **Pyrolator** on the MS-20 and SQ-10 setup the Düsseldorf scene shared.

**Gaps**
- No quotes in Haas's or Goineau's own words exist anywhere I could read. Every quote is a memory told between 2009 and 2022.
- The only machine a band member names is the MS-20 (plus an unnamed "sequencer"). The SQ-10s and MS-50 come from a Sound On Sound writer looking at studio photos, so they're marked as inferred.

The brief I was handed said Paris/Frankfurt and mentioned a Dave Stuart. The band was based in Düsseldorf and I found no Dave Stuart link, so the PDF says that up front.

The last page has five practical ideas for your rig, for example running a 6-step bass line against a 4/4 kick and turning Bartel's tape setup into a Live Session set.

Liaisons Dangereuses, in their own words · 1

B E F O R E Y O U S TA R T

What exists, and what doesn't
Who was who. Liaisons Dangereuses was Beate Bartel, Chrislo Haas and singer Krishna
Goineau. The band lasted from 1981 to 1982 and made one album, released in 1981 and mixed
at Conny Plank's studio near Cologne. Before it, Bartel and Haas made four short cassettes as
CHBB (their initials), each pressed in about 50 copies.
Beate Bartel was a Berliner who, according to Gudrun Gut, trained as a sound engineer at
the broadcaster Sender Freies Berlin. She was in Mania D and the first year of Einstürzende
Neubauten.
Chrislo Haas (1956–2004) played synthesizer and sequencer in the early DAF before forming
the band with Bartel.
Krishna Goineau came from Barcelona, where he had sung with the post-punk group Xeerox
(German Wikipedia, citing Rudi Esch's Electri_City). He was 16 by Bartel's account and 17 by
Sound On Sound's.
A correction to the brief. The band was based in Düsseldorf, with Bartel coming from Berlin:
"ich bin mit Chrislo Haas nach Düsseldorf gegangen, wo wir Liaisons Dangereuses gestartet
haben" (Bartel, Groove 2017, page 4). No source linked the band to Paris or Frankfurt, and no
link to anyone called Dave Stuart turned up.
The big gap. Every first-hand quote here is a memory told decades later, between 2009 and
2022. No interview from 1981–82 could be read. Chrislo Haas died in 2004 and no quote in his
own words was found anywhere. Krishna Goineau has no readable interview either. The best
band source is Beate Bartel, in two interviews (2014 and 2017). The rest comes from the people
around them: Gudrun Gut (Bartel's bandmate in Mania D), Gabi Delgado and Robert Görl (DAF,
Haas's old band), and Kurt Dahlke (Pyrolator, Haas's predecessor in DAF).
On gear, only one band member names a machine: Bartel's line about the Korg MS-20 and "a
sequencer". The claim that the band used several MS-20s, two SQ-10 sequencers and an MS-50
rests on a Sound On Sound writer looking at studio photos. It is marked as inferred in the gear
section.

FOR YOUR RIG

Practical takeaways
These are our suggestions from the sources, not anything the band said.

1. Six against four
The "Los Niños" pull comes from a short sequence cycling against a four-on-the-floor beat. Set a
6-step (or 12-step) bass line against a 4/4 kick, and let it phase back to the downbeat every
three bars. your Metropolix, K-Accumulator, DROID or the SQ10izer module in VCV can each
run a 6-step loop. Try a 3-step accent lane on top so the accents fall in a different place every
bar.

2. Treat the sequencer as a live instrument
Bartel found MS-20 and sequencer "tempi constantly drift apart" live, and Gut said of Mania D:
"We didn't sync it." For your loop fatigue, a deliberate version of this helps. Clock one voice
from Live via the disting NT and leave a second sequence free-running or on a slightly different
division, then pull it back into sync by hand at a section change.

3. Bartel's tape rig is a Live Session set
She ran 4-track stems and played the mix and an effects unit on stage, "the analogue precursor
of Ableton" in her words. In Live, that's 3–4 stem clips per song plus one send effect (a dub delay
or spring reverb). The arrangement then becomes desk moves: mutes, sends and filter rides,
recorded into Arrangement view. This works well for building sections when the loop itself never
changes.

4. The Plank re-amp trick (from DAF)
Plank thought the bare Korgs sounded "so plastic, so cheap", so he played them through guitar
and bass amps in the room. In the box, put an amp and cabinet model (Live's Amp and Cabinet)
on the MS-20-style bass and blend it under the dry signal. Or send the line out of the disting NT
into a real amp and mic it.

5. Keep the palette small
The whole scene ran on one synth family (MS-20/MS-50/SQ-10), a drum source and tape. A track
built from one mono synth voice, one sequencer and a kick, with movement coming from the
mix rather than new parts, is close to how this record was made.
