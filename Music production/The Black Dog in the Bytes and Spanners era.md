# The Black Dog in the Bytes and Spanners era

## Summary
Interview material and musical analysis for The Black Dog around Bytes and its follow-up.
The full PDF text is included.

## Question
> Find all the interview material you can for the group The Black Dog featuring Ed Hanley. All of the material should relate to the releases of the Bytes record, B-Y-T-E-S, and its follow-up. Please limit to these years. Also included are Ken Downey and one other you'll have to look up.
>
> Add any musical analysis of these records. They have a particular charm to their melodies and harmonies.

## Answer
The Black Dog PDF is ready: black-dog-bytes-spanners-in-their-own-words.pdf, 9 pages in the same tagged style as the earlier quote PDFs. The third member is Andy Turner, and I've used the spellings Handley and Downie.

**What I found.** Very little from 1993 to 1995 survives online as readable text.
- The main source is David Toop's *Mixmag* feature from April 1993, which came out with *Bytes*. I could only read it as quoted in a 2023 blog, so those quotes are marked as second-hand.
- In it, Downie talks about where the band name came from. Handley gives the best working-method line in the whole record: "adjusting the filter so that no one point of the song is exactly the same". Toop also describes their small studio room in Mile End.
- From the period press, there's *Music Technology* (December 1993) on Warp, with label boss Steve Beckett on Black Dog, and a *Melody Maker* mention from the same month.

**Music analysis.** No source from the time analyses the notes, so this section is built from later reviews plus my own reading, and each line is labelled as one or the other. I couldn't listen to the records, so treat my reading as ideas to check by ear. The main ones:
- Melodies loop in 3 or 6 beats against drums in 4, so the tune lands somewhere new in the bar each time round.
- Rich chords in the Detroit style.
- Simple, sweet top lines over darker harmony.

I've added three patch sketches for your rig that use these ideas against loop fatigue.

**Gaps:**
- Nothing from Andy Turner in his own words could be found anywhere.
- Nothing from the period names their gear. Downie listed his early setup (an Amiga, a DX7 and an HR16) in an interview around 2009. That's outside your years, so it's only pointed to, not quoted.
- The best *Spanners*-era source is an *i-D* interview from January 1995. It's online only as page scans on Test Pressing, which wouldn't load here, so it's linked in the PDF for you to read by hand. A *Wire* essay that may cover the band also couldn't be opened.

THE MUSIC

Why the melodies and harmonies charm
No 1993–1995 source analyses the notes, and no transcriptions of these records were found. So this
section is built from three things, each tagged: what the band said then, what critics have heard since,
and our own reading of how those features produce the effect. We could not listen to the records
while writing this, so treat every INFERRED line as a hypothesis to check by ear.

1. A loop that is never quite the same twice
Handley's 1993 line is the key: "adjusting the filter so that no one point of the song is
exactly the same,"
SECOND-HAND

"the rhythms have a kind of Afro-Caribbean intricacy, venturing into 6/8 times,
polyrhythm and polymetry" (John Brinker, The Nomadic Subject, 2012). "The odd time signatures of
tracks like 'Yamemm' and 'Focus Mel'" (Slant, undated). "complex, convoluted polyrhythms, that
replace house and techno's usual rigid 4 / 4." (banbantonton, 2023). The closing track of Bytes is
even called "3/4 Heart".
CRITIC, LATER

INFERRED When a melody loops in 3 or 6 beats against drums that phrase in 4, the tune lands on a
different part of the drum bar each time round and only lines up again every 12 beats. A short, simple
melody then sounds like it is turning over rather than repeating, which is much of the "never the same
twice" feeling. Add a slow filter sweep across the whole track, and no two bars are identical even
though nothing new has been written. This is a direct answer to loop fatigue.

2. Rich chords from a Detroit lineage
"splashes of rich harmonic tones and striking, alpine melodies" (Slant). "blossoming
analog chords" and "classical counterpoint" (Brinker, 2012). Wikipedia's Bytes page says the album
"draws influence from Detroit artists such as Derrick May." banbantonton (2023) hears "Caz" and
"Focus Mel" as possible "Transmat / Derrick "Mayday" May tributes," and "Olivine" as close to
Rhythim Is Rhythim's "Salsa Life". Matthew Ingram (written for Woebot, reposted 2023) says of the
earlier "Parallel": "There's something about the chords they chose that sounded very acid-drenched,
somehow alien."
CRITIC, LATER

INFERRED In the Detroit style the critics point to, a single rich chord shape (a minor seventh, minor
ninth or suspended voicing) is often moved up and down as a block, so the colour of the chord stays
the same while the root wanders. That gives harmony that feels both warm and slightly unmoored,
which fits "alien" and "alpine". Check by ear whether the Bytes pads keep one chord shape as they
move.

3. Sweet melody over melancholy
"the sweet melodic lines of »Merck« (Balil) piping into the starry sky" (hhv-mag, 2023).
"the slow piano chords of Balil's '3/4 Heart'" (Resident Advisor, 2023). "celestial choir-like chords. A
simple piano refrain holding it all together." on Xeper's "Carceres Ex Novum" (banbantonton).
Reynolds (2007, on the earlier tracks) hears "a euphoric melancholy" and a riff "played on a church
organ". Hot Press (2001) sums up the sound as "melodic and melancholic quirkiness".
CRITIC, LATER

The charm seems to come from pairing plain, almost nursery-simple top lines (piping,
refrains, single piano lines) with harmony that is darker or more ambiguous underneath. The tune is

4. Modest, slightly naive sounds

Cheap, bright, slightly toy-like voices carry a melody differently from a lush pad: they
sound hand-made, which adds to the charm.
INFERRED

5. Spanners widens the palette
Descriptions from Sykonee (Electronic Music Critic, 2016) and Resident Advisor
(2023): "Raxmus" opens with "jazz and trip-hop inspired opening chords" and is "dub heavy";
"Barbola Work" is "charmingly chintzy techno-salsa"; "Psil-cosyin" "had a strong middle-eastern
flavour" (Ingram); "Chesh" has "nods to modern classical harping"; "Frisbee Skip" has "lambent
melodies". Seven short "Bolts" link the tracks.
CRITIC, LATER

Where Bytes is a set of individual pieces, Spanners is sequenced as one journey, with the
Bolts acting as palette cleansers. That is an arrangement idea worth stealing for an EP.
INFERRED

TRY IT

Three sketches for your rig
Our suggestions, built from the observations above. Starting points, not recreations.

A. The turning-over melody (metre against metre)
Drums in 4/4 as usual (System80 810, or Live).
Melody on a Metropolix track set to 6 or 12 steps, or a Hapax pattern of 3 beats, so it cycles
against the 16-step drums.
Keep the melody to four or five notes from one minor pentatonic or Dorian set; the shifting
alignment supplies the variety.
Variation: give the bass 5 steps and the melody 6 for a 30-step cycle. Or start at 4 and change
the melody length to 3 only in the second half of the track, as an arrangement move.

B. Handley's five-minute filter
Patch one very slow stage from Zadar (several minutes long, single shot, started by a reset at the
start of the track) into the filter cutoff of the melody voice (for example Vult Freak), with only a
small amount so the change creeps.
Add a second, unrelated slow movement (a Marbles X output at a very slow rate, attenuated) to
resonance, so the two never line up.
Variation: use the same slow stage to also widen or narrow decay on Just Friends, so the tone
and envelope age together.

C. One chord shape, moving
In the Hapax chord playground pick one voicing (a minor ninth or a sus chord) and move it as a
block to new roots, rather than following the key.
Put a simple, high single-note melody on top in a plain voice (sine, organ, or a thin square on
Three Sisters or Mangrove), and keep the darkness in the chords underneath.
Variation: use the "Bolt" idea from Spanners: 20 to 60 seconds of the chord shape alone,
unaccompanied, between EP sections.
