# Beating loop fatigue in arrangement

## Summary
Ways to move a loop-based track somewhere instead of leaving a good loop stuck.
Focuses on loop fatigue and the feeling that the music cannot be steered.

## Question
> That's the most common sticking point with modular-centred music, so it's worth pinning down what the stall looks like for you. Which of these sounds closest?
>
> - **Loop fatigue:** the loop sounds great, but you can't see where it should go, so it gets left behind.
> - **Capture:** the patch evolves live, but it never gets recorded in a form you can edit in Live.
> - **Too many options:** with this much gear, you keep patching new ideas instead of finishing one.
> - **Something else:** tell me in your own words.
>
> loop fatigue combined with a perceived lack of flexibility in terms of getting the music to move somewhere desirable

## Answer
That combination usually means the patch has lots of short-term movement but nothing steering it over the long term. Here are three ways to add that steering:

1. **Macro "direction" controls.** Choose three or four things that define a section, such as density, brightness, chaos and register. Route each to one control, for example a DROID P2B8 fader or a Live macro sent as CV through CV Tools and the NT. Moving one control then shifts the whole patch to a new place instead of tweaking one module.
2. **Let Live write the long form.** Draw slow automation lanes in Arrangement view that send CV into the rig, with locators for intro, build, break and peak. Live shapes the minutes while the modular handles the bars. A reference track laid out on the timeline helps you copy its energy curve.
3. **Perform, then arrange by subtraction.** Record a 15-minute multitrack jam while riding those macros, then cut it down in Live. Finding a track inside a long take is easier than growing one from an 8-bar loop.
