# Derrick May as his peers saw him

## Summary
Quotes from Detroit peers about Derrick May, his music and his process from 1986 to 1997.
The full PDF text is included, with each quote tagged by source.

## Question
> I'm a big fan of the musician Derrick May, D-E-R-R-I-C-K, the Detroit techno guy. However, he is a terrible liar and makes up lots of stuff in interviews about how he doesn't use presets or how he's taken samples from a Detroit orchestra, none of which were true. Please can you source original quotes from his peers that talk about him, his music, his process around the years 1986 through to 1997, nothing later. It would be interesting to see what Matt Cogger, Kevin Saunderson, Juan Atkins, Marty Bonds, James Pennington, and others from Detroit had to say about the way Derrick approached making music. Please create a PDF largely using quotations from peers rather than other general information.

## Answer
The PDF is ready: derrick-may-peer-quotes.pdf (9 pages). It's mostly quotes, each with its magazine, date, link and a tag saying how well I could check it. I found less period material than I'd hoped, and I haven't padded it out.

**What turned up from 1986–1997**
- **Juan Atkins**, Music Technology, December 1988: DJing with May as Deep Space, putting an 808 on between records, the three of them sharing Kevin's 909, and May taking Atkins' records to Chicago.
- **Kevin Saunderson**, The Mix, April 1995: "I remember when Derrick started, all he had was a board, a DX100 and a sequencer." Also: "He made 'Rhythim is Rhythim' off that one little keyboard."
- **Short 1988 Q&A answers** from Atkins and Saunderson about May ("Creative", "Mr May").
- **Carl Craig** on Strings of Life, 1995. I could only partly check this one, because the scan of the original page is too garbled to read every word.
- **Others outside Detroit**: A Guy Called Gerald (1990) on May's DX100 and 727, Robert Owens (1997), and DJ Pierre (1995).

**The two claims you mentioned:** both first appear in print in a Music Technology interview from November 1990. "I will never use a preset" is May speaking. The Orchestra Hall string samples are the journalist's own wording, not a direct quote from May. I put that article in a separate comparison section. None of the period peer quotes I found confirms or contradicts either claim.

**Gaps:** I found nothing from 1986–1997 by Matt Cogger, Marty Bonds, James Pennington, Thomas Barnett, Eddie Fowlkes, Blake Baxter or Mike Banks. I left out 5 items from after 1997 or undated, including Michael James's account of where the Strings of Life piano came from. They're listed at the back with a one-line note each. The Face's May 1988 Detroit feature is online only as page scans that I couldn't read. If you can get at those scans, or a 1988 Music Technology issue, I can add whatever is in them.

What exists, and what doesn't
Very little period testimony from May's Detroit peers survives online in text form. Most of it sat in
UK print magazines such as The Face, Music Technology, NME, Mixmag, Black Echoes and
Record Mirror, and much of that is only available as page scans or behind a paywall. The quotes
in this document are the ones that could be read and checked. They are fewer than you might
hope, and nothing has been added to fill the gaps.
Two period sources describe May's method directly: the 1988 Music Technology interview with
Juan Atkins, and Kevin Saunderson's 1995 interview in The Mix. A third, Music Technology's
November 1990 interview with May himself, is where the "no presets" line and the Orchestra Hall
strings account first appear in print. It is reproduced in the comparison section so the peer
quotes can be read against it, with May's words and the journalist's narration kept apart.
No 1986–1997 quotes were found from Matt Cogger, Marty Bonds, James Pennington (Suburban
Knight), Thomas Barnett, Eddie Fowlkes, Blake Baxter or Mike Banks about May. The last section
lists where they may still be found in print.

What was printed at the time about May's own method
The two claims you mentioned both trace back to this article. May's quotes and the writer's narration are kept apart.
MAY / WRITER "Techno Rhythim", interview by Simon Trask, Music Technology, November 1990, pp. 38–42. muzines.co.u
k/articles/techno-rhythim/452

Presets (May speaking)

"I've got basslines that only sound right with particular sounds. I can actually hate a song
because the sounds aren't right"
"And I will never use a preset; that is against the law where I come from."
The Orchestra Hall strings (writer's narration, not in quote marks in the original)

"The distinctive string sounds May has been using in his Rhythim Is Rhythim tracks since 'It Is
What It Is' have their origins in samples he took himself at Orchestra Hall in Detroit"
The sentence continues: "where the city's symphony orchestra rehearses and performs." This is Trask's narration of what
May told him. It is not a direct quote from May.

Gear and working method (May speaking)

"I started out with a couple of S900s, a DX100, a Poly 800, a Mirage, an SQD1, a 909, an 808, a
727 and a Fostex 260 four-track"
The sentence continues in the original.

"my concept has always been to get the feel from all drum machines simultaneously, not just
one feel from one drum machine."
"I play all my own music. There's no engineer that sits back and makes the music for Derrick
May"
"Sampling things, then recording them onto cassette, sampling them again, recording them
onto cassette again"
How this lines up with the peer quotes. Saunderson (1995) and Gerald (1990) both put a DX100 at the centre of
May's early sound, and Atkins (1988) describes a small, shared pool of drum machines. None of the 1986–1997 peer
material found here mentions presets, Orchestra Hall, or where the "Strings of Life" piano and strings came from.
That question is mostly argued in sources from after 1997 (see Gaps and exclusions), so these quotes can't settle it
either way.
