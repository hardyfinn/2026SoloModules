# Note weightings for house, gospel, funk, soul and reggae

## Summary
Suggested scale-degree weight profiles for house, gospel, funk, soul and reggae.
Profiles are informed estimates rather than measured corpus data.

## Question
> This looks great. Ahead of the next build, im interested to know what a house music profile would be. A gospel profile wpuld be good. Funk? Soul? Reggae?

## Answer
There are no published note-count studies for these styles, so like Detroit Dorian they'd be *designed* profiles. I'd build them from the rock corpus (the closest measured data) plus each style's habits. Here's what I'd propose:

| Profile | Scale | Strongest notes | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **House** | Dorian / minor | 1, ♭3, 5, ♭7, with 9 (2) held | Minor 9th stabs, hypnotic |
| **Gospel** | Major | 1, 3, 5, then 6, 2, 4 | Bright, IV-chord pull, a little ♭3 and ♭7 as blue notes |
| **Funk** | Dorian / blues | 1, ♭7, ♭3, 5 | One-chord vamp; the major 3rd rubs against ♭3 |
| **Soul** | Major | 1, 5, 3, 6, 2 | Pentatonic, with ♭3 and ♭7 bending in |
| **Reggae** | Major | 1, 5, 3, then 2, 6 | Pentatonic and bass-led, few 4s and 7s |

Each could also suggest an accent setting. The best example is reggae's "one drop", which puts the landing note on beat 3 (Strong 3 of 4).
