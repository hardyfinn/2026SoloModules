RANDRUM

A bag of drums and a handful of dice. A drum sample player for VCV Rack, by Haer.

1. Install

  1. Drop Randrum-2.3.0-mac-arm64.vcvplugin into ~/Library/Application Support/Rack2/plugins-mac-arm64/.
  2. Restart Rack. Randrum is under the brand Haer, or search "Randrum".

Newer builds always appear at github.com/hardyfinn/randrum, Releases, latest.

2. First sound in one minute

  1. Right-click Randrum and pick Load folder.... Choose a folder of WAV or AIFF files, such as a folder of hats, a whole kit, or a whole library: subfolders are included. You can also drag the folder (or any sample inside it) from Finder onto the screen.
  2. Patch a clock into TRIG. A VCV Clock 1/16 output, or any gate sequencer, works.
  3. Patch OUT L into your mixer or Audio module. With nothing in OUT R, OUT L is the mono mix.
  4. You'll hear a different file on each hit, in a pattern that repeats every 8 hits and changes slightly each time round. That's the default: SPREAD full, LOOP 8, MUTATE 10%.
  5. Turn MUTATE fully down. The pattern locks. Turn PITCH RND up and the same pattern gets pitched hits, still repeating.

3. The dice, and why the pattern can stay still

Each hit uses eight dice, one each for: which sample, pitch, decay, start, level, pan, reverse, and whether it plays at all. A die is just a number between 0 and 1.

The knobs don't roll anything. They only say how far each die may push. PITCH RND at zero means the pitch die is ignored. At full it can move the pitch 12 semitones either way. That split is the main idea:

Turning a RND knob rescales the rolls you already have. A locked pattern stays the same pattern while you make it tamer or wilder. That gives you a "how much" control over randomness that doesn't throw away a groove you like.

LOOP and MUTATE

With LOOP at 1 to 16, the dice for each hit are kept in a ring of that many steps. Hit 1 uses step 1's dice, hit 2 uses step 2's, and so on round the ring. With LOOP fully CCW (free), every hit gets fresh dice and nothing repeats.

MUTATE is the chance, on every hit, that each die in the current step is rolled again. It works like a Turing Machine's lock knob, but per die, so a hit might keep its sample and only get a new pitch.

Above: an 8-step loop with MUTATE around 15%. Bar height is one die (say, pitch). Orange steps were re-rolled that time round; teal ones repeated.

MUTATEWhat you hear
0%Locked. The same 1-16 step pattern for ever. Use REROLL to get a new one.
2-8%A pattern that grows over minutes. Good for long Detroit-style hat parts.
10-25%Recognisable but restless. Each pass has a few new moments.
50% and upThe loop barely holds. Close to free mode.

VARIETY: fewer sounds, spread out

VARIETY (the small knob at the bottom right) sets how many different samples the loop uses, from 1 to all. At 3, an 8-step loop uses three sounds and repeats them, but a sound never sits on the step next to itself, even around the end of the loop. You get A B A C B C A B, not A A B B C C. Each sound gets an even share of the steps. The letters around the LOOP ring on the screen show which sound each step uses.

Fewer sounds makes a loop easier to hear as a phrase, like a drummer with a few drums instead of a whole kit. Try LOOP 16, VARIETY 3, MUTATE 5%: the three sounds stay, but where they fall slowly shifts. VARIETY works in rnd pick mode. With LOOP on free, each hit picks one of the VARIETY sounds, never the same one twice in a row.

REROLL (button or gate) rolls the whole ring at once. RESET makes the next hit step 1 again. Patch your bar or pattern reset into RESET so the loop lines up with the rest of the track.

Patterns: memory, morph and ratchets

The column on the right (new in 2.3, which made Randrum 28 HP wide) turns Randrum into an arrangement tool.

ControlWhat it does
A B C DFour pattern memories. A dim light means the slot holds a pattern. A bright light marks the pattern the loop came from.
SAVEPress SAVE (it lights), then a letter, to store the loop there. Press SAVE again to cancel.
jump / morphjump: pressing a letter switches to that pattern at the start of the next loop, so it lands in time. The letter blinks until then. morph: the letter becomes a target, and MUTATE copies the target's dice instead of rolling new ones. The loop drifts toward the target one die at a time, at a speed set by MUTATE. Press the letter again to go back to random mutation.
PAT CV0-10 V picks A to D (2.5 V each), so song sections can come from Ableton or a stepped CV.
RATCHET + CVThe chance a step plays a roll of 2, 3 or 4 quick repeats, spread evenly until the next trigger, each a little quieter. Which steps roll is part of the dice, so rolls repeat with the loop. Every repeat also fires HIT.

The screen shows it all at the top of the LOOP panel: LOOP A means the loop is pattern A, A~ means MUTATE has moved it since, B next means a jump is waiting, and > B 63% is a morph 63% of the way there. Steps that roll get a yellow ring.

ARRANGEA track from four patterns

  1. Find a groove you like with MUTATE at 0 and store it in A. Press REROLL until you hear a busier one and store it in B. Store two more in C and D. Only the dice are saved, not the knobs, so the RND knobs, CHANCE and RATCHET still shape each pattern live.
  2. Verse: jump to A, MUTATE 0.
  3. Build: switch to morph, press B, and set MUTATE to about 10%. Over 8 to 16 bars the groove turns into B, step by step.
  4. Break: jump to D and bring RATCHET up for rolls. Return: jump to A for the drop.
  5. From Ableton: send a CV clip into PAT CV (0, 2.5, 5, 7.5 V), so the arrangement is written on Live's timeline.

5. Reading the screen

  • Top line: the folder and how many files it has, then the PICK mode (and CHOKE when it's on).
  • Second line: the file that just played, in orange, or in purple when CHANCE skipped it. Then its pitch, decay, level, pan, and REV when backwards.
  • Waveform: the last file, flipped when it played backwards. The orange line is the start point, the yellow curve is the decay, and white lines are playheads.
  • Strip under the waveform: one cell per file. Teal cells are inside the SPREAD window, and the orange cell is the one that played. Turn SAMPLE and SPREAD and watch the window move.
  • LOOP ring: one dot per step. Filled dots will play at the current CHANCE and hollow ones will be skipped. Bigger dots are louder (when LEVEL RND is up). The orange dot is the step that just played. Turn CHANCE slowly and watch dots go hollow one at a time: that's the order in which hits drop out, and it stays the same until they mutate.

6. Starter patches

DETROITLiving hats

  1. Folder of closed hats (or closed and open hats together). Clock 1/16 into TRIG.
  2. LOOP 16, MUTATE 3%, SPREAD about 30%, PICK walk.
  3. LEVEL RND at 40% for velocity, DECAY at 11 o'clock with DECAY RND at 30%.
  4. CHANCE at 85% so a few steps drop out. Choke on if the folder has open hats.
  5. Variation: patch a slow LFO into DECAY CV so the hats open up over 16 bars.

AUTECHRESelf-mutating perc

  1. A folder of clicks, glitches and found sounds. Clock 1/16 (or a Euclidean pattern) into TRIG.
  2. LOOP 7 or 11 (an odd length against 16-step bars, so it phases), MUTATE 12%.
  3. PITCH RND 60% with whole semitones off, START RND 50%, REV 20%, PAN RND full with both outputs patched.
  4. Patch a slow random voltage into MUTATE CV, so the pattern alternates between holding still and breaking up.
  5. Variation: PICK cycle with SPREAD at 5 files and LOOP 4. The sample order and the dice drift against each other.

MIXKick that ducks everything

  1. One Randrum with a kick folder, SPREAD at zero (always the same kick), DECAY full.
  2. Patch ENV into the CV of a VCA (inverted) on your pad or bass, or into a sidechain-able compressor. Every kick pushes the rest down, and short decays give short ducks.

CALL AND RESPONSETwo Randrums sharing a beat

  1. Randrum A: snares and claps, CHANCE 50%, LOOP 8, MUTATE 5%.
  2. Patch A's SKIP into Randrum B's TRIG. B has rims or toms.
  3. Every hit A drops, B plays. The two parts never collide, and their pattern moves together as A mutates.

MODULATIONThe drum picks the sound

  1. Patch IDX into a filter cutoff or a reverb send on the same part, or on a different one.
  2. Each file now has its own colour, and the colour follows the sample choice, including mutations.

7. Arranging with it (beating loop fatigue)

Randrum is built so a part can repeat and still go somewhere. Some ways to steer it across a track:

  • Lock for the verse, open for the break. MUTATE at 0 while a section plays, then raise it for 8 bars at the transition, then lock again. You land on a new pattern that grew out of the old one.
  • Ride CHANCE as a density fader. Because the dice are locked, lowering CHANCE drops hits in a fixed order. It's a build or breakdown control that thins the part musically instead of randomly.
  • REROLL on phrase starts. Patch a trigger every 32 bars into REROLL for a fresh pattern each phrase, or press it by hand when a part goes stale.
  • Map from Ableton. Send CV from Live (CV Tools through the disting NT, or VCV's MIDI-CV from a Live MIDI track) into MUTATE CV and CHANCE CV, and automate them on the arrangement timeline. Live then holds the plan, and Randrum does the variation.
  • Same folder, different jobs. Two Randrums on one folder with different SPREAD windows (one low, one high) give you two related parts from the same kit.

8. In your rig and with your other modules

  • Clock: drive TRIG from your Seuperclidean or Metroplex outputs for patterns, or from a plain 1/16 clock and let CHANCE make the pattern.
  • Reset: patch the same reset you send to your sequencers, so LOOP 16 lines up with the bar.
  • Velocity: a Marbles-style random or a sequencer's accent lane into VEL. LEVEL RND then adds variation on top.
  • Hardware drums: the HIT, SKIP and ENV outputs can go out through the disting NT's outputs to trigger the System80 810 or open a filter in the case, so the dice also play your hardware.
  • Kits: keep kits as sibling folders (for example Drums/hats-dusty, Drums/hats-bright). Right-click, then Next folder, or pick from Folders next to this one, to swap a kit without leaving the patch.

Good to know

  • The folder path and the loop's dice are saved in the patch, so a locked pattern comes back exactly. The audio files aren't copied into the patch, so keep the folder where it is. If it moves, the screen says MISSING.
  • Up to 1024 WAV or AIFF files per folder, counting subfolders, up to 30 seconds each, in any bit depth or sample rate. MP3 and other compressed formats are skipped. Files starting with a dot are ignored. Turn off Include subfolders in the right-click menu to load only the top folder.
  • If a folder won't load, the screen says why. "macOS blocks Rack" means Rack needs permission for that place (often Documents, Desktop, Downloads or an external drive): System Settings, Privacy & Security, Files and Folders (or Full Disk Access), then allow VCV Rack 2. "audio engine stopped?" means Rack isn't running audio, so check the Audio module's device.
  • Files play in alphabetical order of their path across SAMPLE, so each subfolder stays together, so naming them 01-, 02- and so on lets you arrange the folder from soft to hard, or dark to bright. WALK mode then moves smoothly between similar sounds.
